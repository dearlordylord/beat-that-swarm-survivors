# Current balance engine and bot audit

Read-only inspection of the Survivors source at `f1f21c5` (2026-10-25). No campaign or benchmark was run. The working tree already contains unrelated edits in `examples/survivors/social/`; they were left untouched.

## What currently controls difficulty

| Mechanic | Current rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary enemy population | The per-run active-slot budget starts at 96, adds one per 50 simulation ticks, and caps at 300 in solo. Each dormant slot is considered on one of six tick phases, then admitted when its stable seed is below the current budget. This is a population schedule with staggered admissions, not a simple “spawn N enemies every second” timer. | [`game.bend` active limit and admission](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L263), [`dormant_scheduled_for`](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L465) |
| Enemy selection and HP | Stage selects a stage-specific roster. The number of possible variants expands from 3 toward 6 as `wave_tick` advances. Base enemy health is multiplied by `100 + tick/180 + 4*(tick/3600)^2 + stage*18`, in percent, then by the selected player’s Curse modifier. Boss variant 30 has a separate 10,000,000 base HP floor. | [`encounters.bend` roster and health](../examples/survivors/encounters.bend#L30), [`game.bend` enemy construction](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L263) |
| Mode/time | Standard run length is 36,000 ticks (10 minutes); Hurry is 18,000 ticks and doubles `wave_tick`, accelerating the spawn/HP schedule. Hyper adds 35% to the ordinary spawn budget. Endless keeps the periodic boss schedule but has no timed victory. | [`game.bend` run length and wave clock](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L349), [`game.bend` spawn modifiers](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L470) |
| Incoming damage | A contact pulse begins with armor-reduced base damage, then adds elapsed-time pressure (`tick/3600`), a crowd term capped at 6 (`hit/3`), and a final-minute ramp capped at 24. Boss contact has a separate large-hit term. Stage hazards add another 2–6 damage on their sparse pulse schedules, outside that pressure function. | [`game.bend` damage curve](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1140), [`encounters.bend` hazards](../examples/survivors/encounters.bend#L162), [`game.bend` applied HP update](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1182) |
| XP/progression | Enemy drops contain 2–6 base XP, depending on variant. XP need is `40 + 6*level + level²/3`. Physical pickup credits the whole stored pile; bonuses are applied to credited XP. | [`persistent-xp.bend`](../examples/survivors/persistent-xp.bend#L3), [`game.bend` drop and level curve](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L261), [`game.bend` XP bonus](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1140) |

Some tuning is expressed as formulas spread across `game.bend` and `encounters.bend`, rather than a single pressure-curve table/configuration. A future controller can extract these into a pure Bend config without changing gameplay semantics first. Seeded roster selection, tick curves, hazard schedules, and weapon tables are deterministic and inspectable.

### Co-op balance as implemented

Co-op is one shared world with at most two player records. `core_join` accepts exactly one guest before tick 0. Each record retains personal HP, character, weapon/passive ranks, Arcana, meta and movement; `projection` overlays shared clock, XP, level, kills, gold, stage, mode, bosses and run seed. Thus this is not two independent solo runs joined in presentation. [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L159) [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L300)

Ordinary spawn budget is multiplied by the number of connected, living players and capped at 500. A disconnected player is projected to zero HP for simulation; a dead player no longer contributes to the count. The dedicated boss slot remains on its existing schedule. Enemy HP, contact damage, elapsed time and boss rules do not receive a blanket `×2` multiplier. [`party-difficulty.bend`](../examples/survivors/party-difficulty.bend#L4) [`game.bend`](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1615)

There is a less obvious co-op coupling to preserve or make explicit: the spawn slot chooses a player view using `seed % 2` (falling back to the nearest living player). The scheduler uses that view’s passive slot 14 and Arcana 5 in its spawn-budget modifier; newborn HP uses those plus character 6. With two living players and different personal bonuses, spawn eligibility and newborn HP can therefore vary by slot parity; this is not a party aggregate formula. Active enemy movement chooses the nearest player. [`game.bend`](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1532) [`game.bend`](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1621)

The two players’ attacks are both planned into the shared projectile pool. XP pickups are physical and assigned once to the nearest eligible collector; each collector’s own XP bonus is applied before credit is accumulated into shared XP. Level-up rewards are requested from every living player and generated against that player’s own loadout. A chest reward is assigned to its collector. [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L338) [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L393) [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L405)

## Existing laws and their limits

- `party-difficulty-LAWS.bend` has one law: exact product scaling and cap at 500. It does not pin down the surrounding spawn schedule, the policy for per-player Curse, the cap’s player-count transition, or player-count effects on expected XP and survival. [`party-difficulty-LAWS.bend`](../examples/survivors/party-difficulty-LAWS.bend#L4)
- Spawn-policy laws cover geometric off-screen admission and newborn lifecycle; they are correctness laws, not a pressure or density target. [`spawn-policy/LAWS.bend`](../examples/survivors/spawn-policy/LAWS.bend#L6)
- Combat-policy laws cover saturating subtraction, contact admission and invulnerability. They do not assert that the current time/crowd/last-minute curve is the intended curve. [`combat-policy/LAWS.bend`](../examples/survivors/combat-policy/LAWS.bend#L4)
- Persistent-XP laws cover helper-level pickup/merge accounting. The actual ECS path has a fixed 400-slot XP pool. When no slot is free, new drops merge into an accumulator/selected existing pile; this is bounded-pool overflow behavior, not an unbounded number of entities. Merges use `U32` arithmetic, and the law file explicitly does not claim natural-number conservation under wraparound. [`pools.bend`](../examples/survivors/pools.bend#L10) [`game.bend`](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L970) [`persistent-xp/LAWS.bend`](../examples/survivors/persistent-xp/LAWS.bend#L22)
- No current balance law establishes monotonic pressure with time, an acceptable 1p/2p survival envelope, XP pacing, damage-source attribution, or a victory-rate target. This audit located no such law; it did not rerun proof commands.

## What the bots and native batch can actually do

The native offline batch is the best existing seam for cheap, repeatable experiments. `offline-batch.bend` imports the real game and the deterministic `offline-policy.bend`; it runs without a renderer, frame parsing, Node, HTTP or classifier. It handles upgrade decisions through the fixed heuristic and movement through a deterministic spatial-memory/risk-grid planner. It does not learn from previous runs. [`offline-batch.bend`](../examples/survivors/offline-batch.bend#L1) [`offline-policy.bend`](../examples/survivors/offline-policy.bend#L7)

Current configuration includes count, tick horizon, base seed, fresh/advanced/both profile, process-vs-Bend execution, worker count and runtime threads. Global index assigns `seed + index`; “both” alternates profiles deterministically. The run startup itself is code-fixed: fresh is character 0, stage 0, Arcana 0, standard mode; advanced is character 2, stage 0, Arcana 9, standard mode. The current batch interface has no co-op, party size, stage/mode matrix, per-player setup, or policy selector. [`offline-batch.bend`](../examples/survivors/offline-batch.bend#L78) [`native-batch.sh`](../tools/survivors-bot/native-batch.sh#L8) [`bot-actions.bend`](../examples/survivors/bot-actions.bend#L132)

There are other controller paths, but they answer different questions. `native-full-run.bend` is a single fixed qualification policy/preset with hand-authored build priorities. `native-bot.bend` is the visible window experiment with a bounded file-command protocol. The optional `jev` classifier can advise on legal upgrade or movement candidates, but simulations pause while it responds; it is not part of the native batch and is not an evolving optimizer. [`native-full-run.bend`](../examples/survivors/native-full-run.bend#L40) [`native-bot.bend`](../examples/survivors/native-bot.bend#L7) [`tools/survivors-bot/README.md`](../tools/survivors-bot/README.md#L28)

The existing batch metrics are run-end HP, level, XP, kills, gold, choices, XP-pile count, uncollected base XP and wall time. There is no damage-taken total, damage source breakdown, time-series HP/max HP, enemy population trace, damage dealt/DPS, individual co-op outcome or XP pickup/detour trace. The co-op hit journal is a bounded (64 ticks / 512 rows) cosmetic confirmed-projectile-contact witness; its own schema says it is not damage/kill authority, and it does not provide received damage attribution. [`offline-batch.bend`](../examples/survivors/offline-batch.bend#L45) [`coop-hit-journal.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-hit-journal.bend#L6)

## Historical runtime and result caveats

The recorded 100-run native cohort used seeds 100–199, 1,800 ticks per run (30 simulated seconds), 50 fresh + 50 advanced, 8 workers, and completed in 231.54 wall seconds. The reported per-run median wall time was about 16.5–16.7 seconds; the 231.54 seconds is aggregate campaign wall time with workers, not one simulation’s runtime. One fresh run ended at tick 1,778 in phase 3; the other 99 were bounded observations. It is explicitly marked early-game only. [`survivors-native-bots.json`](survivors-native-bots.json#L11)

That receipt is not evidence for today’s exact tuning: it says its workspace included XP-cost tuning and embeds a `game.bend` hash different from the current file (`366aa0…` in the receipt vs `359391…` now). The report also says its old JS cohort used another controller/backend, so the reported JS/native wall times do not establish a speedup comparison. [`survivors-native-bots.json`](survivors-native-bots.json#L12) [`survivors-native-bots.json`](survivors-native-bots.json#L57) [`survivors-native-bots.json`](survivors-native-bots.json#L85)

Metric labels need careful interpretation. `offline-batch` sets `outcome: "terminal"` for every phase `>= 3`; it therefore collapses phase 3 defeat and phase 4 timed victory. `status: "completed"` means the batch emitted a completed summary, not that the game was won. Use `phase` and tick: phase 3 is defeat; phase 4 is the game’s endpoint-victory state. The existing full-run qualification specifically expects phase 4 with HP 0 at 36,000–37,000 ticks, so record that endpoint rule rather than assuming ordinary “alive at 10 minutes” semantics. [`offline-batch.bend`](../examples/survivors/offline-batch.bend#L32) [`game.bend`](../examples/survivors/game.bend#L1129) [`native-full-run.bend`](../examples/survivors/native-full-run.bend#L164)

The 30-second horizon cannot support claims about survival to the 5/10-minute endpoint, final-minute pressure, evolved-build strength, victory rate or co-op balance. HP alone is net health after damage, healing, level choices and max-HP changes; it is not damage taken. Kills are not damage dealt or DPS. Treat nonterminal horizon stops as right-censored runs, not wins or “survivals” for a full run.

## Concrete seams for the next balance loop

1. **Extract the tuning inputs into one pure Bend policy module** (for example, `multiplayer-balance.bend`) while preserving current values first: spawn budget from tick/wave tick, living players, mode/Curse; enemy HP from variant/tick/stage/Curse; base contact damage and its time/crowd/end-ramp terms; hazard pulse. Give callers a fixed `BalanceConfig` and return named components, not only a summed HP delta. Decide explicitly whether Curse is per-slot parity, party maximum, party average, or another approved rule before altering it.
2. **Add a true native two-player batch harness around `coop-core`**, not two solo runs and not two players multiplexed through `offline-batch`. Start one shared seeded core, attach a second player before tick 0, maintain two independent policy memories, call `core_step` with both movements, and service each required upgrade round with that player’s own actual offers. Initially run classifier-free. Compare against solo controls at the same seed/config and use identical player policies to isolate party-size effects; later cross policy pairs (XP-seeker / safety-first / damage-first) to observe interaction. `core_join`, `core_step` and `core_choose` are existing Bend entry points. [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L306) [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L494) [`coop-core.bend`](../examples/survivors/coop-core.bend#L530)
3. **Expose a narrow Bend-native telemetry record per tick/run.** At minimum retain player ID, tick/phase, HP and max HP, damage components (enemy contact/projectile, pressure addition, hazard), healing, invulnerability, living enemy count/spawns/kills by variant, XP spawned/physically collected/remaining, level/choice/weapon progression and terminal reason. Preserve raw denominators and censored status. The current player hit flag and aggregate HP do not contain enough information to reconstruct these causes afterward.
4. **Freeze the control panel before candidate balance changes:** source commit and source hashes; Bend/compiler/backend; per-run seed list and seed assignment; ticks and terminal policy; stage, mode, character, meta, Arcana, party count and player build; fixed controller module/hash; classifier disabled; worker/thread configuration; and exact outcome/telemetry schema. Keep the same panel and paired seeds for baseline and candidate. Add separate panels for fresh and advanced profiles, solo and co-op, and stage/mode sweeps only after the first paired result is understood.
5. **Pin laws to semantics, not desirable win rates.** Add pure laws for cap and monotonicity within legal game horizons, player-count transition (0/1/2 living), death/disconnect effect on future spawns, spawn budget never changing existing enemy HP, component damage summing to applied damage, invulnerability/hazard behavior, and XP value conservation through pickup/overflow. Integration scenarios should separately pin timed endpoint, early defeat, disconnected/one-player co-op, and both-player death. Keep victory-rate and pressure targets as measured acceptance thresholds in the frozen experiment, not universal mathematical laws.

This gives a clean sequence: prove the pure balance function contracts, compare instrumented solo and shared-world co-op batches, then use deterministic policy variants to expose exploits or weak styles. Only after that should a classifier be offered as another frozen controller arm. The optimizer/improver is an experiment loop over recorded hypotheses and one changed parameter set at a time; current bot code has no autonomous learning step.
