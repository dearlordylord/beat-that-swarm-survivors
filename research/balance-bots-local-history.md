# What Hapsland has already explored regarding game balance

**Purpose:** Preserve a local history of the methods and boundaries of previous experiments, useful for the Survivors bots' balancing plan. **Audience:** Developers of Survivors, authors of balance experiments and owners of game design. **Status:** Research Note; the suggestions below are not accepted as requirements for Survivors. **Authority:** Analysis of existing Hapsland sources and reports. This is a testament to previous prototypes, not a game specification or proof of the balance of Survivors. **Expected use:** Use to select a method, implement a future bot cycle, and check which old numbers don't apply. **Lifecycle:** Preserve as evidence of local sources and previous experiments; update when the corresponding laboratory changes.

## Short conclusion

There are two different layers to local history. The old `MechanicsStudy.py` and `RoadStudy.py` are independent analytical models, not game code. The newer lab API in the `canonical-defense/lab` can run an experiment through an actual Bend game model, capture the configuration, and reproduce the result. This is the best example of experiment architecture for Survivors.

From this you can borrow condition control, pairwise comparisons, multiple metrics, pre-declared contexts, holdout and exact replay. You cannot transfer the parameters of a tower game, its wave frequency, health points, or conclusions about the winners. The lab does not contain adaptive bots, a learning optimizer, cooperative scenarios, or human research.

## What's in the repository

### Real model laboratory: `canonical-defense/lab`

The guide describes the development in the order of “visual playability first, then balance” and requires comparing a complete playthrough without the ability with a playthrough with it with the same input. Acceptance must distinguish between proven invariants, finite traces, and visual verification ([playability procedure, lines 36–45](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/PLAYABILITY.md:36>)).

`runGameExperiment` creates the world through Bend `Game.create_scenario`, sets the seed, budget, map and load settings, executes the action schedule, automatic shots and a limited number of game ticks/business events ([game.ts, lines 200–291](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/game.ts:200>)). The layer compares observations of the actual game with normal business runtime; recording contains the identities of source, configuration, trace and replay, and reexecution discards the changed data ([game.ts, lines 94–117](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/game.ts:94>), [294–303](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/game.ts:294>)). This is a useful example: the bot should play on the same simulation engine as the product, and not on a simplified simulator that may diverge from it.

The laboratory can compare the declared baseline/candidate, the four options of `baseline, A, B, A+B`, and search only against the transmitted final list of plans with an explicit list of untested options ([game.ts, lines 306–349](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/game.ts:306>)). Search is not an optimizer and does not change the game itself. A separate API marks contexts `tuning` and `heldOut`; the search works according to tuning, and `evaluateHeldOut` evaluates the already selected pair, does not select the winner based on holdout results ([lab README, lines 89–97](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/README.md:89>)).

The recording contains separate game and business observations: health, expenses, queue of finds, real hits, completions and callback time. The guide specifically warns that the same seed is not enough to match every random outcome if the action changes the order in which operations are issued; you need to check the coupling of outcomes ([lab README, lines 81–93](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/README.md:81>)). For Survivors, this means fixing not only the seed, but also the way randomness is assigned to events/entities, so that changing the bot's behavior does not shift the entire RNG stream and make the comparison noisier. Seed pairing remains valid; it does not guarantee identical encounters or a sustained reduction in variance.

### What exactly did the tower laboratory show?

The current `game-example.ts` announces nine contexts - seven tuning and two held-out - and nine plans: an empty baseline, individual abilities, their combination, upgrades and alternative purchases. There are 81 combinations in total; automatic simulation is limited to 20–200 ticks and 2,000 business events ([game-example.ts, lines 52–95](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/game-example.ts:52>)). However, the authors explicitly assigned this grid `educationalQualification: "unqualified"`: the previous short horizon does not establish the full benefit of physical tower hits or holdout balance. To test the stop script, the test confirms that all nine candidates finish before hitting, completing review, and delivering ([game-example.ts, lines 113–132](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/game-example.ts:113>), [focused test, lines 783–825](</workspace/typescript/jev/scripts/game-balance-lab.test.mts:783>)). This means that 81 is the declared volume of the matrix, and not 81 successful confirmation of the balance.

The guide specifically notes qualified narrow examples of full ability path and partial delivery to Stop, but they do not prove global balance and are not a study of player training ([lab README, lines 55–71](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/README.md:55>)). Most of the lab's testing establishes reproducibility, bounds, budget, and specific effects—it's the quality of the measurement tool, not an assessment of whether it's fun to play ([focused test, lines 42–110 and 665–689](</workspace/typescript/jev/scripts/game-balance-lab.test.mts:42>)).

### Old analytical models

The `MechanicsStudy.py` explicitly calls itself an analytical calibration, not a game loop. It models three stages of Hapsland service, abstract demand seeds and static resource allocations; real identities, revisions, callbacks, multiple actors and failures are excluded. The script itself states that the transitions in this toy model do not prove Hapsland behavior ([MechanicsStudy.py, lines 1–27](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/bend-tower-defense/MechanicsStudy.py:1>)). The saved result contains 8,064 lines for six synthetic scenarios, three loads and 64 seeds; metrics - coverage/delivery, losses and occupancy, with Pareto comparisons ([MechanicsStudy.py, lines 67-79 and 193-223](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/bend-tower-defense/MechanicsStudy.py:67>), [result metadata](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/bend-tower-defense/mechanics-results.json>)).

Useful ideas from this work: fix the workload before comparing policies; use paired seeds; announce selection criteria in advance; do not collapse coverage, delivery and cost into one “magic” score; consider unfulfilled demand as a failure rather than a disappearing load. The script specifically checks for equal demand between policies and does not allow the idle policy to win due to an empty denominator ([MechanicsStudy.py, lines 5–6, 29–41 and 281–283](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/bend-tower-defense/MechanicsStudy.py:5>)). This is a portable methodology, not portable mechanics.

The `RoadStudy.py` models another three-stage system with fixed batches, 7 scripts, 8 layouts and 32 seeds (1,792 lines). As results, it separately stores coverage, danger, losses and capital, checks the preservation of demand and Pareto-frontier. The result itself is clearly labeled as an independent analytical model without parity with a Bend game; even the estimates for the native configuration are called analytical bounds, not race ([RoadStudy.py, lines 1–28, 43–84 and 215–289](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/bend-tower-defense/RoadStudy.py:1>)). Therefore, the observation “speeding up the upper stage is automatically beneficial” does not carry over to Survivors; Only the need to check subsequent bottlenecks and alternative targets is transferred.

## Application to Survivors: A Plan Based on Local Experience

These are recommendations for the next lab design, not a description of work already done.

1. **Save the real simulation path.** Add a headless input to the same Bend simulation that is executed by the browser/native game; exclude visualization and UI from measurement. Compare state/observations from a common core, like game lab does. The simplified Python/JS model should only be used for a rough search of hypotheses and should be explicitly marked as surrogate.
2. **First set up metrics contracts and a time profile.** For each fixed duration of the run, record time windows: time and number of appeared/active/dead enemies; incoming damage and damage received by the player; current and lost HP; XP on the ground/collected XP; time of each level-up; DPS/weapons/passives; threat peaks and recovery windows; time/cause of death or victory. The pressure graph should show exactly the time series, and not just the average or the “survived/died” outcome. First check that the telemetry matches the game state and tick time.
3. **Declare a compact fixed family of bots.** Start with deterministic controllers with different strategies: stationary evasion/survival, movement towards XP cluster, priority of safe XP, aggressive collection of near XP, and controller with memory of abandoned XP piles. Add oracle/pathfinding only as a top score, not as a regular player. Everyone has a version of the policy and decision; The classifier is an optional separate layer, not a condition for mass runs.
4. **Compare changes in pairs.** For a source-config pair, use the same seed and comparable randomness of enemies, drops, levels and upgrades. If actions change the order of random calls, use independent RNG streams or keyed randomness for the subsystem/entity/tick event; Do not consider “the same seed” as evidence of the same encounter. Record source/config/policy identity, traces, and reproducible record of each run.
5. **Separate tuning and confirmation.** Select parameters on a pre-announced set of seeds, cards, characters, weapons and game stages. Record the candidate, then compare it with the baseline on individual seeds/contexts that were not used in the selection. Don't use holdout to reconfigure and don't call multiple authorized fixtures population statistics.
6. **Show trade-offs and pressure curve.** In addition to lifetime, display individual indicators: XP pickup rate/remaining experience, average and peak mob flow, damage per player, kills, levels and frequency of skill selection. Compare the windows of the curve: is there an early safe development, an increase in pressure, honest short breaks and a rather difficult late stage. Do not choose a winner based on one total number without the explicit decision of the designer.
7. **Introduce a separate co-op set of contexts.** Run one and two players on the same scenarios and seeds, with an explicit mode for the distribution of XP, levels/pauses, revives and loot; add asymmetrical skills, low contribution/AFK player, and single player loss as separate test cases. The report should show both team outcomes and the contribution of each: survival time, kills/damage, XP pickup, level timing, down/revive. Solo playthrough is not evidence of cooperative balance.
8. **Leave bot improvement controlled.** Retrospective - a report on the trajectory and unsuccessful decisions, from which the analyst proposes a new version of the policy. The new bot is first registered as a separate strategy, goes through the same paired and holdout checks, and only then replaces the previous one in the set. There is no learning or self-substitution in the current Hapsland lab: its search only iterates through the listed plans, and the failing/untried cases remain visible ([lab README, lines 89–97](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/README.md:89>)). This is the right boundary for the first cycle of Survivors.
9. **Separate machine results from human test.** Once the simulation is set up, confirm the difficulty profile with short human runs: expected/actual difficulty, cause of death, salience of XP decisions, and sense of respite. The old guide explicitly states that satisfaction, learning and global balance do not follow from automatic routes ([PLAYABILITY.md, lines 66–80](</workspace/typescript/jev/prototypes/canonical-defense/lab/PLAYABILITY.md:66>)).

## What can't be carried

- Tower parameters, timings, budgets, damage and context names Hapsland is a different game and a different causal model.
- Synthetic loss-network results from `MechanicsStudy.py` / `RoadStudy.py` - not the races of a real Bend core; they should only be used as hints about experimental design.
- The statement “same seed = completely identical random encounters” is incorrect when the order of event generation has changed; this is expressly stated by the laboratory.
- The current `canonical-defense/lab` does not provide Survivors bots, self-training, co-op simulator or proof that the balance is close to Vampire Survivors.
- The success of a deterministic bot does not establish enjoyment, readability, or fairness for a human; requires separate human viewing.

## Practical next step

For Survivors, it is worth first determining which properties of pressure and cooperation are considered desirable, then making a replay-able headless experiment API on the actual Bend world and four or five simple versioned bot policies. The first iteration should be diagnostic: baseline graphs, paired runs and a list of counterexamples for XP/deaths, without automatically changing the numbers. Once the results are explainable and repeatable, a limited parametric search can be run with independent holdout and human candidate verification.
