# Photon Fusion and FishNet: projectiles, AoE and contact confirmation

Retrieved **October 25, 2026** from primary sources. For Photon, Fusion 2 and samples of **Projectiles Essentials 2.1.1** (July 10, 2026) and **Projectiles Advanced 2.1.1** (August 6, 2026) were reviewed. For FishNet - release **4.7.4** (October 16, 2026), documentation and sources from the `4.7.4` tag. The code was not transferred to the project; SDKs were not installed and samples did not run. [Photon Essentials](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/technical-samples/projectiles-essentials), [Photon Advanced: download](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/technical-samples/projectiles-advanced/download), [FishNet releases](https://github.com/FirstGearGames/FishNet/releases), [FishNet source: PredictionProcessor](https://github.com/FirstGearGames/FishNet/blob/4.7.4/Assets/FishNet/CodeGenerating/Processing/Prediction/PredictionProcessor.cs).

## Output for Bend browser host/Iroh

The transferable idea is to separate **input commands, game ticks, projectile lifetimes, confirmed combat outcomes and visual prediction**. The command number is suitable for ordering/confirming input, but in itself does not determine at what tick of the world it was executed by the host. In Fusion, the simulation has its own fixed tick and is re-executed from the server snapshot; FishNet collects `Replicate` on `TimeManager.OnTick`, stores the queue and history of tick data, and replays entries upon reconcile. Therefore, the Iroh message queue with FIFO order does not set the correspondence to `input → worldTick`: this correspondence must be written explicitly and checked on the host. [Fusion: controller code](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/concepts-and-patterns/networked-controller-code), [FishNet: creating prediction code](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs/guides/features/prediction/creating-code/controlling-an-object), [FishNet source](https://github.com/FirstGearGames/FishNet/blob/4.7.4/Assets/FishNet/CodeGenerating/Processing/Prediction/PredictionProcessor.cs).

In our combat simulation, your own shot must interact with enemies and already incoming projectiles, assessed at a compatible game time. A separate prediction is that only the owner does not know the future distant shot: it can kill or move the target earlier and thereby change not only the damage, but also the recipient of the next piercing contact. This result remains provisional for the client until the host confirms it or corrects it. This is a finding for our model and not a guarantee of any of the SDKs. In Fusion, the visual of a distant shot can specifically go according to Remote Render Time, while its own visual can go according to Local Render Time; The Essentials page explicitly warns that shots fired in the same tick are therefore visible in different time phases. Smooth overlap of images does not establish equality of combat worlds. [Fusion projectile timing](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/technical-samples/projectiles-essentials#about-projectiles-timing).

### AoE, hits and visible results

Fusion API can give the server the composition of a group of targets within the radius at the time that the shooting client saw: `OverlapSphere(... player: Object.InputAuthority, options: HitOptions.SubtickAccuracy)`. The server-side game code then filters the targets and applies health/effects. For multiple hitboxes of one enemy, you need to explicitly define deduplication at the level of one target. Hitting the future point of explosion of an incoming projectile is a different temporary rule than overlap on the input/fire tick: detonation should be confirmed based on the world and the contact ticks of the projectile, and not automatically roll it back to the moment of the shot. The API does not specify this product rule. [Lag compensation query](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/manual/advanced/lag-compensation).

Projectiles Advanced shows networked `Explosion` with damage radius, `Damage Area` with damage over time and a small networked ring buffer of hit events. The `Hit` structure shown contains action, damage, relative position, direction, instigator and fatal flag, but does not show shot/event ID, tick or target lifetime ID. This is an example of response synchronization, not a promise of deduplication, full AoE prediction, or exactly-once delivery. [Advanced architecture: damage](https://doc.photonengine.com/fusion/v2/technical-samples/projectiles-advanced/architecture).

In FishNet lag-compensation projectile sample, server-only collision HP changes; the local client copy only shows the effect, and the way to synchronize health is left to the game. Therefore, local contact/AoE candidates should not be passed off as final damage. For particles and damage numbers, it is useful to associate the provisional and confirmed display with the same event identity. In prediction replay, FishNet distinguishes between `Ticked`/`Replayed`, but the replay state is suitable for suppressing repeated cosmetics within the simulation, not for deduplicating individual network hits. Separate `NetworkCollider`/`NetworkTrigger` callbacks work with prediction for primitive shapes; FishNet warns that `OnStay` does not return Unity `Collision`. [Projectiles](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs/guides/features/lag-compensation/projectiles), [replicate states](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs/guides/features/prediction/creating-code/understanding-replicatestate/using-states-in-code), [NetworkColliders](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs/guides/features/prediction/using-networkcolliders).

## Practical contract for our model

1. Store the input as `{actorLifetime, commandSeq, intendedWorldTick, payload}`. `commandSeq` serves command ordering, ACK and deduplication; simulation tick is assigned/validated separately. For multiple shots/bullets from one input add `ordinal`. Late-input policy must be explicit: assign a future tick, accept/reject the previous one, or adjust the forecast; Receiving several messages in one frame does not in itself say anything about their game ticks.
2. The logical life of the projectile should be associated with at least `{ownerLifetime, commandSeq, ordinal}`; in one server event indicate `worldTick` where the contact occurred. Do not use a physical pool slot as an ID. Keep the network projectile state compact: trajectory type/parameters, world birth tick and initial parameters, then only changes (hit, bounce, stop, target change). This is consistent with the data-buffer approach, but does not involve porting the Fusion/FishNet API.
3. The host must resolve contact/pierce/AoE-recipient set against a compatible world state once. A confirmed event carries `{eventId, shotId, impactTick, targetLifetime, pulseOrdinal, point/radius, action/damage}`; the client can immediately draw a preliminary projectile/zone, but checks HP, kill, pierce and recipient set against the event and ignores the repeat of `eventId`. Each legitimate repeated beat/pulse has its own ordinal. `targetLifetime` protects against ID/slot reuse.
4. Keep canonical combat state and render-only offset separately. With reconciliation, the combat truth/trajectory changes, and anti-aliasing only masks the movement of the picture. For your own piercing shot, along with the goal, you need to take into account those future events that we already know; an unknown remote input cannot be considered known just because its message will arrive later.

This is an architectural application of published patterns to Bend + Iroh; it does not require replacement of transport, ECS or general simulator. In particular, a complete rollback of all entities is not a necessary consequence of the fact that Fusion/FishNet can rewind hitboxes or repeat inputs of individual network objects.

## Regression cases for subsequent changes

- Delivery of one or several fire inputs in one packet, permutation/duplicate packets and delay: the same pair of `commandSeq → intendedWorldTick`, without turning the FIFO into a clock.
- Your piercing projectile against a moving enemy with a late ranged shot that kills/knocks that enemy back before contact; check the confirmed target and the next remainder of the pierce with the host timeline.
- Multiple shots of the same type in one tick, multiple pellets in a command and pool-slot reuse: IDs do not collide when buffer wrap or redelivery.
- AoE at the border of the radius, two collider zones of one enemy, the enemy is killed before the impact tick, target with reused ID, several legal aura pulses; recipients/damage match the host event.
- Visual effects for replay, late hit-event and repeat event ID: confirmed damage is applied once, sound/particle is not played on each replay.
- Load and buffer capacity at high projectile/enemy counts; Separately look at the overflow/deletion of old records, cost reconciliation and visibility of the event to new/lagging observers.
