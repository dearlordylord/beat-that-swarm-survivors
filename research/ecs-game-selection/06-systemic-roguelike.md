# Hypothesis 6: systemic grid roguelike as a Bendvy ECS showcase

**Initial report, hypothesis 6 out of 10.** Comparing *Caves of Qud*, *Cataclysm: Dark Days Ahead* (CDDA) and compact traditional roguelike as forms of game. First, I distinguish between the maximum expressiveness of ECS and the best first clone. The game facts below are linked to developer sources and open source; The ECS model, estimates, and proposed cross-section are the findings of this study. The Bendvy repository is read for reference only and has not been modified. The code, benchmark, or game did not run.

## Two different results

- **Maximum potential within this hypothesis:** *Caves of Qud*. The player, enemies, and NPCs share mechanical families—abilities, body, health, equipment, affiliation; impacts can change the actors and material of the world. This potentially results in strong rules overlap. Sources confirm gaming coverage, but do not prove that Qud is globally superior to other candidates as an ECS consumer.
- **Most feasible first clone:** a narrow *traditional roguelike* with one map, an explicit objective and few rules. This is not a clone of the full *Qud* or CDDA. Of the pair, I'd take Qud as a guide for the mechanics, but limit the game to one level of ruins and one short run. Such a slice may be similar to *Qud*, but until research is completed it cannot be said that it is already recognizable as its clone.
- **CDDA** is particularly strong for items, long activities, needs, injuries, vehicles and the environment, but its simulation breadth creates substantial content and host scope before a simple dungeon loop can demonstrate ECS benefits.

Recommendation for general comparison: leave the system roguelike among the strong candidates for ECS coverage, but do not yet declare Qud the global winner and do not consider the depth of the simulation as a reason to choose it as the first clone. If this option is chosen, start with a single Qud-like layer and test the value of independent queries before adding a wide survival loop.

## Primary facts about games and their cycle

The official CDDA page describes turn-based survival: the player begins by exploring the surrounding area and searching for food, water and safety, then chooses long-term survival, skill development, crafting and other goals. She also calls collecting resources and fighting or running away from creatures. In the source code in the project repository, the turn is implemented as an ordered cycle: fields and items are updated, explosions are processed, monsters/NPCs move, then character turn processing is called and the weather/morale is updated. Activity documentation describes long-running activities that can be interrupted and continued; they have `start`, `do_turn`, `finish`, serialization and cancellation behavior. These are real examples of phases, lifetime and repeatable state, but the original architecture does not prove the need for ECS. ([CDDA](https://cataclysmdda.org/) official page, [turn loop in `do_turn.cpp` on committed commit](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/do_turn.cpp#L734-L780), [activities](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/doc/PLAYER_ACTIVITY.md#L16-L44) documentation)

The official *Caves of Qud* page describes exploration of procedural regions, over 70 mutations, the ability to corrode walls or melt them into lava, full-fledged NPCs with levels, skills, equipment, factions and body parts, as well as faction relationships. The official material also talks about physical, social and historical simulations. Freehold's research paper on the generation of mythical biographies clarifies only one part: historical entities (places, objects, sultans) are represented by data, and historical events change their properties. This is **not** evidence that Qud's game runtime is ECS; the article describes its history generator, not the current game architecture. ([official Qud press kit](https://cavesofqud.com/press-kit/), [Freehold Games article on history generation](https://www.freeholdgames.com/papers/Generation_of_Mythic_Biographies_in_CavesofQud.pdf))

*Qud* thus provides the greatest mechanical coverage: changing the body, abilities, relationships and environment changes the player's actions. CDDA provides a broader end-to-end survival experience, but its own design materials emphasize realistic simulation, resource gathering, and goal variety. ([CDDA gameplay design](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/doc/design-balance-lore/design-gameplay.md), [CDDA player-experience design](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/doc/design-balance-lore/design-user-experience.md))

## What Viewing CDDA Open Source Reveals

This audit is limited to a few files on commit `4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc`, it is not an audit of the entire game. In the studied places, the model is object-oriented: `Creature` - a class with a move handler, coordinate, map of body parts and effect methods; `Character` is inherited from it. The typed `item_location` accesses an item through one of several types of location - a character, map, vehicle, container, or temporary crafting inventory - and is documented as a handle, which can be invalidated during copying and other operations. This is an architectural observation from sources, not a claim that no ECS subsystems exist anywhere in the repository. ([`Creature` API](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/creature.h#L257-L343), [`Creature` state and effect access](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/creature.h#L646-L691), [`Creature` position and body](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/creature.h#L814-L830), [`Character`](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/character.h#L527-L550), [`item_location`](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/item_location.h#L42-L68))

CDDA has its own event bus with `subscribe`/`unsubscribe`/`send`; adding an effect to a character sends a typed event about receiving the effect. The turn code treats object types and world systems separately. This is a useful example of events and processing order, but the existence of an event bus does not in itself prove either independent ECS readers or Bendvy's schedule API. ([`event_bus.h`](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/event_bus.h#L15-L37), [`Creature::add_effect`](https://github.com/CleverRaven/Cataclysm-DDA/blob/4d87d4992c58d7839e95ff3535fd54683802b2dc/src/creature.cpp#L1998-L2001))

In a short vertical slice, the player makes one moving choice - walk, hit, use an ability/item, or go through an obstacle. Then he sees the result in the map, the state of the actors, the resources spent and the event log. The ending conditions are clear: pick up the relic and leave the map, or lose if you die/fail your turn. It offers a testable product cycle, but the specific commands, relic target, and completion form are a slice design, not statements about the original games.

## Proposed ECS model (design output)

The main sign of success is not the number of entities, but several independent rules that select common fields among different families of objects. Example check: the same impact system reads `Position + Wet` from the enemy, player and creature, and another selects only `Burning + Health`; an equipped item can define abilities, and the loss of a hand or temporary mutation changes the available actions.

| Family | Possible composition | Why do requests overlap? |
|---|---|---|
| Player, enemy, neutral creature | `Position`, `Health`, `Faction`, `BodyPlan`; optional `AIIntent`, `Wet`, `Burning`, `Poisoned`, `EquipmentSlots`, `Mutations` | Movement/damage general; AI - only for the corresponding subset; the status system refers to different kinds of beings; missing the component changes the behavior. |
| Item in the world, carried item, equipment | `ItemKind`, `Position` or `CarriedBy`, `Durability`, `Charges`, `Weapon`, `Consumable`, `Armor` | Pickup, ownership, use and wear select overlapping properties. An equipped item has an effect through a connection with the owner; the lying item is applicable in the same inventory flow. |
| Action/effect with lifetime | `Duration`, `DamageOverTime`, `Owner`/source, `Target` | The lifetime and application of the effect are different systems; the source is needed for logging/award or deletion. Do not create separate entities for purely decorative magazines. |
| world | dense mesh `Terrain/Material/Field` and a small set of interactive props (for example a door, a trap) | The actors' positions refer to the map; field reactions and wall destruction produce changes readable by AI and interactions. It is better to keep the main table of tiles as a separate grid host service/resource, rather than turning each cell into an ECS object. |

The connections that really justify the relation API are: `OwnedBy` for an item in the inventory, `EquippedBy/Slot` for an equipped item, and `Target` or `Source` for an effect/attack. They need to be checked by asking “all character items”, moving the item from the map to the owner and clearing the link when the item is removed/the creature dies. If the relationship is needed only for rendering or the component itself already stores the same value, such a relation will be an artificial API cover.

One possible order (not a description of the Qud/CDDA source):

1. Host translates the button/key into one `PlayerIntent`; only the first intent per turn is allowed into the authoritative state.
2. Movement/interaction checks the adjacent cell and updates the position or creates a `Attack`/`Interact` observation.
3. Creature action systems select requests for components `AIIntent`, `Faction`, `Weapon`, `CanMove`; AI-targeting is a limited map service.
4. Contacts/effects apply/remove statuses, consume charges and change health; a separate reaction of the world changes nearby cells (for example, acid corrodes a wall).
5. Status/environment tick reduces duration and applies local effects; death is published once and puts cleanup/drop in deferred commands.
6. The barrier makes structural changes visible to independent readers of combat, log, level target and visual response. The external success output appears only after commit.

Here the transaction and snapshot should help the real cycle: abilities are not wasted on a prohibited move; if there is an error, the damage system world returns to its launch boundary; the log gets committed result; the state after the move can be observed or exported. If the slice doesn't check for failure/retry/observation and has no need for persistence, don't add these mechanisms just for the sake of the API. Runtime snapshot cannot be interpreted as a launch checkpoint.

## Contract valuation

The ordinal expert assessment within this hypothesis is: 0 - absent, 1 - weak, 2 - noticeable, 3 - defines the game. For "Outside ECS Workload", a higher value means more work outside the ECS (this is a risk, not a benefit). These are assessments of concepts, not measurements or global rankings of ten candidates.

| Criterion | *Caves of Qud* | CDDA | Short grid roguelike | Base |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Combination | 3 | 2 | 2 | Qud's abilities, body parts, factions, and items create the widest combinations; generic rogue is usually easier. |
| Overlapping queries | 3 | 2 | 2 | Qud involves a general simulation of creatures and interactions with the world; in the small dungeon there are more similar actors. |
| Lineup changes | 3 | 2 | 2 | Mutations/new limbs and changes to Qud states can change the set of actions. |
| Events and phases | 2 | 3 | 2 | CDDA explicitly documents interruptible ongoing activities and an orderly turn loop; Qud's event-rich gameplay is a design output from simulations, not a confirmed runtime design. |
| Relationships | 3 | 3 | 2 | Items, equipment, factions, source of effect and goals make the relationship playful for the first two; A small dungeon will often be limited to possession/target. |
| Suitability of the first cut | 1 | 1 | 3 | One complete dungeon is natural for a roguelike; a very narrow Qud/CDDA cut may not convey the characteristic width of the original. |
| Load outside ECS | 3 | 3 | 1 | Procedural world/content, field simulation and paths are able to dominate Qud and CDDA; The compact map allows for a straightforward simple solver. |
| Novelty regarding arena | 3 | 3 | 2 | Inventory/equipment/factions/statuses/moving game significantly expand the swarm arena; generic arena-dungeon with combat-only not so much. |

The main choice depends on the goal: if you need to show a maximum of different ECS contracts over the next few prototypes, a Qud-like system is the best guide. If you need to quickly get a complete game consumer, the basic dungeon is simpler, but it will reveal less than an ordinary simulation of statuses, relationships and environment.

## Vertical cut that can be held

One map screen about the size of a room, one controllable character, two enemy types, several items and one exit. One short task: raise the relic from the ruins and leave the level. No campaign, generation of thousands of historical entities, crafting, transport and full body simulator.

- The player and opponents share `Position + Health + Faction`; enemies are distinguished by `AIIntent` rather than the monolithic `Kind` switch.
- One item is a weapon, the other is a consumable effect; both can be picked up and thrown, weapons can be equipped via relation.
- Two player abilities: for example, short movement through an obstacle and caustic gas. The latter destroys **one type** of wall in the grid service. This retains the Qud-recognizable ability to change environments without building generic solver stuff.
- One component status (for example `Burning`) is added to both the enemy and the player, decreases over ticks and can cause death. Death creates one loot/drop and event; The message, exit target, and rendering effect read the event separately.
- One real choice: open the door safely and waste your turn, or destroy the wall and immediately come under enemy vision. The player sees the route change, the enemy's reaction and the outcome of the status. Victory means leaving with a relic, defeat means death; restart repeats the scenario with a fixed seed.

The small card makes A*/BFS a replaceable utility or host service. Do not include complex dynamic navigation, overworld, matter simulation, or permanently alive NPCs outside the active map. Pathfinding/field/grid solutions are not automatically owned by ECS and are not provided as ready-made services in Bendvy.

Slice is only recognizable as a **Qud-inspired roguelike** until the mutation character build and social/environmental chains are added. If recognition cannot be achieved without them, the honest goal is a complete original traditional roguelike with a clear Qud-reference, and not a statement about a faithful clone.

## Bendvy fit now

In a normal registration application, the main input: systems are executed in a given order, the explicit barrier determines the visibility of deferred commands, component queries accept `Args:Data`/`Out:Data`; at the same time, the payload of the components themselves can be affine `Type` with owner-preserving bindings and Data projections. This makes it possible to represent heterogeneous and potentially affine values, but they must be transferred through the operations provided by the owners; Don't plan to freely assign a common affine value to different entities or callback generations. The slice should keep reusable arguments/observations in Data form, and any required `Type`-affine runtime memory should be processed through the already existing captured Local/query path with the owner returned. ([ordinary App guide](../../../bendvy/docs/reference/ordinary-app.md#build-the-registrations), [capture-query section](../../../bendvy/docs/reference/ordinary-app.md#retain-an-affine-capture-across-query-runs))

Component world, queries, system capabilities, deferred commands, relation and typed event/readers are suitable supports. Browser arena already connects ECS movement, targets, damage and cleanup, but uses a composite `Body` with `kind`; a new roguelike needs to prove independent components/queries and relation lifecycle. ([arena](../../../bendvy/examples/arena/README.md), [relations contract](../../../bendvy/docs/parity/relations.md), [typed events](../../../bendvy/docs/parity/typed-data-events.md))

Constraints affecting the slicing design: systems execution is ordered and does not imply free concurrency; deferred entity changes must be manifested at an explicit barrier; ECS rollback does not return external IO, and some capture changes fail; basic/graph save clearly refuses when structural commands are incomplete and snapshot is not a complete execution checkpoint. Game renderer, input, tile map, collision/pathfinding and level generator - host/feature code with separate contracts, not built-in ECS services. For the first slice, it is reasonable to do without saving the live session; fixed seed and snapshot after commit are useful for monitoring, but are not a substitute for restoring the entire queue/readers/runtime. ([evaluation contract](00-evaluation-contract.md), [SPEC save and failure decisions](../../../bendvy/docs/SPEC.md#clock-and-save-corrections--authorized-after-74), [ordinary App barriers and failure](../../../bendvy/docs/reference/ordinary-app.md#attach-the-schema-and-run))

## Objections and refusal criteria

- **A short AoS update will do the trick.** With one player and two enemies, it’s easier to store actor structures and a separate map. ECS is useful only if real requests overlap: the same status works on different creatures; some systems update equipment, others select weapons; the presence of an ability changes the available moves; Cleanup of relationships is reliable. If this is not the case, the usual array is simpler and more honest.
- **The main difficulty may lie with ECS.** For Qud, this is the interaction of changeable walls, local fields, procedural generation and authorized content; CDDA has long-term activities, subject containers, needs, the world and their rules. The first version should support one grid system and not pretend that the entire tile solver is expressed in ECS.
- **You can't hide a monolith behind a flag.** One `Actor{kind,flags,all_stats}` and a centralized switch for move/combat/status will again give the arena demo form. The check fails if the query is only syntactically broken, but all callbacks are called by one main handler and the components do not change in the game.
- **Relations and events must have a causal role.** If weapon possession is not involved in attack, removal or surveillance, a separate `EquippedBy` relation is not justified. If death only reads one callback, check if the independent reader actually serves the task. Failure/rollback is only useful when there is a reproducible illegal move/error that should keep the state fair.
- **The term “clone” may promise too much.** One stripped-down floor is not CDDA, and a level without a mutable character/world may be insufficiently similar to Qud. Keeping the loop, choice, visible result and win/loss authentic is more important than listing mechanics outside the cut.

Abandon a Qud-like option as a first clone project if compelling mechanics require general pathfinding, a long campaign, a large content list, or complex field/material simulation before verifiable ECS queries. Refuse the ECS claim if a strong vertical slice, after removing the grid and narrow content, can be expressed in one cycle over small structures without inconvenient dispatch and loss of necessary modifications. In this case, choose a compact traditional roguelike (clear choice, one level, clear victory/death), and leave Qud/CDDA as the source of mechanics for the next expansion.

## Counter-test after hypotheses 1–5, 7–8

Qud **has not established that it demonstrates ECS better than the alternatives**. Its breadth is evident in the descriptions of game mechanics, but Qud's runtime sources have not been examined, so it is unknown how often these mechanics actually overlap through common queries. The strongest objection is [hypothesis 7](07-space-station.md): SS14's official ECS guide and code show systems that work with common station and crew entities; this is more direct evidence of compliance with ECS than for Qud. [Hypothesis 8](08-modular-ships.md) also describes a dense chain of crew, tasks, modules, power, damage, fire and ship connectivity, but its data is game descriptions and devlogs, not an audit of a commercial runtime architecture. There are fewer mechanics for an FTL-like version, but fixed rooms and a short route make it more convincing as the first game slice.

- Hypothesis 1 gives Brotato a shorter completed loop: waves, weapons, loot, store decision and next wave. Magicka-like combat from hypothesis 4 is a more compact ECS check; Noita, like Qud, carries the risk of mesh simulation.
- In Hypothesis 2, shapez gives explicit examples of component storage and filter systems, although the factory graph remains a separate solver. This is strong evidence for query/composition, but not sufficient to infer greater overall breadth. A Mindustry-like hybrid can cross mobile actors with manufacturing and defense.
- Dwarf Fortress/RimWorld from Hypothesis 3 could provide comparable or greater breadth across workers, quests, needs, items, buildings, and social status; the work scheduler and cellular environment add work outside the ECS. The macro RTS of Hypothesis 5 links economy, production, movement, ownership, targeting and combat, but navigation and army command may dominate the first prototype.
- SS14 is probably stronger than Qud in terms of proven ECS integration, and Cosmoteer is comparable in the composition of game mechanisms; For both, the first completed clone is riskier. In the ship report, FTL stands out as the cheaper bounded slice, but Brotato and Magicka also look easier for the first result.

**What will change the recommendation:** Qud can be promoted to the leading ECS candidates if the runtime study or prototype shows three end-to-end cases: the general status of the player and NPC, independent requests for possession/equipment and their effect, as well as an impact on the world that changes the passability and affects both AI and the outcome. I would shift the recommendation to SS14 or Cosmoteer if their system-component map shows more independent intersections at a recognizable small cycle; for the first clone - to FTL, Brotato or Magicka, if their slices confirm a recognizable game with less load on the host. Qud should be lowered if these checks require a large grid solver first, or if a one-room slice does not convey recognizable mechanics. Global selection still requires the same system-by-family map for the remaining candidates.
