# Tower defense as a Bendvy ECS consumer

**Hypothesis 9 of 10.** Compact lane-defense is compared based on *Plants vs. Zombies*, branching improvements *Bloons TD 6* and action-hybrid *Dungeon Defenders*. Facts about the mechanics are taken from the official EA/PopCap manual and the publisher's game pages on Steam. The ECS model, slice volume and conclusions about the suitability of Bendvy are design conclusions, not a description of the original engines. The internal architecture of commercial games is not confirmed by these sources. It is proposed to make a clone with your own names, rules and graphics.

## Conclusion

The best **first recognizable clone** is a small original lane-defense game in the spirit of *Plants vs. Zombies*: The player places defenses in several rows, spends resources on different plants, chooses a weak point and survives predetermined waves. Fixed rows allow you to make do with enemy progress along a route instead of pathfinding. This slice gives the player's true decision and a closed win/lose outcome without a physics engine.

Within the TD group, *Dungeon Defenders* has **maximum potential coverage**: in addition to waves, defensive structures and enemies, the game's official list includes controllable heroes, class abilities, direct combat, weapons and equipment, drops, pets, tower upgrades and co-op. It's a rich surface for composition and relationships, but a significant portion of the cost will come from action-RPG, 3D movement, collision, host, and networking. This is the upper bound of this particular hypothesis, and not the winner of the overall comparison of ECS candidates.

Recommendation within this hypothesis: **start with a short lane-defense game, then add one meaningful upgrade choice**. *Bloons TD 6* is a good source of ideas for improvements, but not a reasonable amount of clone: Ninja Kiwi itself describes 25 types of towers, three upgrade paths each, activated abilities, heroes, over 70 cards, co-op and meta improvements. The improvement graph is mostly static data and balance; The mere fact of having a DAG does not make it an ECS task.

For the first clone, lane-defense remains convenient: it has a short strategy with an end goal. For the stated goal of **maximum ECS**, this hypothesis is weaker than directions where player actions frequently change entity composition or long-lived relations between roles matter. In report 04 Magicka gives inter-family statuses and spell delivery; 10 Baba - explicit addition and removal of behavior following a rule change; 08 FTL/Cosmoteer - crew, rooms/modules, tasks and goals. TD can easily end up being just an arena plus a grid: towers are usually static, the map is compact, and upgrade branches can remain as strings in the catalog.

*PvZ* works as a first clone form, *Dungeon Defenders* shows more potential reach, and *Bloons* is useful with the limited idea of branching upgrades. This comparison does not imply that the original games use ECS.

## Proposed ECS model

Objects should be separated by data and requested behavior, not by a central `kind`-switch. Possible set:

- **Towers/Plants:** `Position`, `Lane`, `Health` (only if they can be destroyed), `AttackCooldown`, `Range`, `Cost`; the shooter adds `ProjectileAttack`, the moderator - `SlowAura` or `SlowOnHit`, the resource generator - `ResourceProduction`, the disposable trap - `DetonateOnContact`. Systems for placement, shooting, resource generation and maintenance of damaged structures require different intersections of components.
- **Enemies:** shared `Position`, `Lane`, `Health`, `ForwardProgress`, `MoveSpeed`, `Reward`; a specific composition adds `Armor`, `Shield`, `Fast`, `SplitsOnDeath` or special behavior. A single movement system can update base fields for multiple families, and protection and separation effects are enabled by the presence of its own components.
- **Shells:** `Position`, `Velocity`, `Lifetime`, `Damage`, `HitRule`, if necessary `Pierce` and `StatusOnHit`. This is a separate family from towers and enemies; a common status effect can affect different entities if they have the required position/speed/effect.
- **Temporary states:** for example `Slowed{ticks}`, `Poisoned{ticks}`, `Burning{ticks}`. The timer system reduces deadlines on all compatible entities, and the effect systems only poll the carriers of a specific component. If the implementation requires a separate `if enemy/tower/projectile` branch for each status, composition has not been achieved.
- **Resource and wave:** sun remainder, wave queue description, match mode, seed and timer state are best stored as resource or application state if they do not need their own entity-handle, lifecycle or relationships.

Relationships have useful but limited roles. The projectile can store the `FiredBy` relation to the tower for hit attribution, damage reporting, and correct link clearing when selling or deleting the tower. The tower can only have a `CurrentTarget` relation to the enemy if holding the target is actually part of the rules; When the target dies, the link is cleared at an explicit structural barrier. The field cell does not have to be an entity: `Lane + Column` is usually simpler and clearer. Consequently, TD has useful links, but relationships are not the central reason for choosing the game.

Event `EnemyDied` can have several independent readers: one awards the reward, the second counts the remainder of the current wave, the third informs the host about the visual effect. `ProjectileHit` can separately run damage, status and hit tracking. The event reader must receive its own cursor/stream according to the existing contract so that the reward does not "swallow" the event for the wave controller. These reactions are needed as independent game logic; If events are used only for the sake of demonstrating the API, they should be removed.

The schedule has visible game boundaries: process placement/upgrade commands; apply structural commands to the barrier; update enemy movement and fire towers; create projectiles and apply the desired barrier again; update projectiles/collisions; apply damage, death events, rewards and cleaning; check the wave and outcome. Preparation, combat, wave clearing, and the final screen should limit which systems work. The match result and UI are published after the corresponding ECS transaction is successfully completed. Specific system placement, registration requirements and barriers must correspond to the real API; automatic parallelism is not assumed.

An option with improvements can keep the turret handle, but add/remove behavioral components on the obvious border: the basic shooter gets the “double shot” (`BurstAttack`) or “slowing charge” (`StatusOnHit{Slow}`) branch. The enhancement catalog and valid edges remain static data; The ECS reflects the applied turret composition. This is how a meaningful change in the composition of the world appears. Checking cycles in a common graph, a huge balanced catalog and meta-progression are not needed.

## Vertical slice and player's decision

Slice: One card, three rows of eight squares, one base currency, several selected plant types, two enemy roles, and four short preset waves. Host draws the grid and accepts clicks; Bendvy maintains the state and rules. There is no physics engine and free pathfinding: the enemy goes to the base along its own line with one progress scalar.

At the beginning, the player selects a limited set of available plants and sees how much resource he has. During the match, he spends a resource on an income generator or defense, chooses a line where there is not enough fire, decides whether to strengthen the line with a cheap slow or buy a shooter, and chooses the moment of one improvement. After each wave, he receives a reward or sees lost lives. Victory comes after the fourth wave and the destruction of all its enemies; defeat - when the total lives of the base are exhausted after a breakthrough. The player can restart the short game and check a different resource allocation. This leaves a recognizable cycle of “planning - placement - wave reflection - result”, and not a sandbox without a goal.

Four waves, two enemies and a small map - a design proposal, not numbers from the original game. The recognition of a clone is determined by the decision to place in rows with a finite resource, and not by the accuracy of copying numbers, characters or levels.

## New relative to the current Bendvy and #64

The current [arena](../../../bendvy/examples/arena/README.md) already shows moving enemies, pickup objects, bolts, pulses, chains, damage and clearing. But its gameplay is stored in a composite `Body` with a numeric `kind`, and the interactive demonstration uses direct-query orchestration. A TD clone must add individual overlapping components, multiple system registration, economic decision before and during the wave, spatial placement, temporal effects, and observable phases. If it repeats the general `Body{kind,...}` or reduces the loop to one update with a long dispatch, the novelty for ECS is small.

The [Canonical Tower Defense integration plan for #64](../../../bendvy/docs/parity/defense-plan.md) is complete as a **plan**, not as a gameplay integration. It requires keeping the original reducer as the sole business authority and, in the first copy, placing the complete `CanonicalWorld` into one resource; the first version does not mirror game actors into ECS entities. The plan itself explicitly states that copying and execution are not qualified and are dependent on remaining preconditions, including auditing the full core #61 and real host adapters/traces. Therefore, #64 does not prove that a game using ECS components is ready, and does not implement this candidate. But in essence this is the same defense genre: a separate PvZ-like clone can duplicate the visual purpose of the future product. It is worth doing only if you need an independent ECS consumer with different compositions, and not a second tower-defense project for the sake of quantity.

Compared to #64, this study suggests a different consumer and a different question: not "does the integration adapter preserve the exact behavior of the original reducer" but "whether the individual components, queries, and events are useful for a game where builds, effects, shots, and waves change state independently." This is not an executable proof, nor is it an execution of #64.

## Hostile cross-check: how TD loses to ECS maximum

A three-row slice gives the player more solutions than the current arena, but does not in itself prove a strong ECS composition. In the variant from this report, the enemy `Slowed` is almost certainly selected only for enemies; upgrade branches mainly change the set of tower attacks; the only useful long-lived connection is the optional `FiredBy`. This is more modest than the runtime rule-property change in Baba’s proposal and than the crew–room–job or module–target relationships in FTL/Cosmoteer. Therefore, the grid, economics, and explicit outcome make TD a more complete strategy game, but not automatically a more ECS-performing one.

Three possible improvements can be built without `EntityKind` branching, if you first lay down the general game systems and data:

| Improvement | Reusable components and systems | Prerequisite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Targeting versus ammunition:** a tower prioritizes threats and can intercept an enemy projectile flying toward it. | `TargetPriority` on the tower changes data for general target acquisition; enemies and intercepted projectiles are shared by the `Position + Faction + Targetable + Damageable`. The same fire, projectile movement/hit, damage and death systems work on both targets. | Add an enemy that fires projectiles and make the projectiles separate `Damageable/Targetable` entities. This is not yet available in the minimal PvZ-like wave. |
| **Breakthrough Charge:** Improved projectile continues to fly after being hit. | `PierceBudget` changes the behavior of the common projectile-contact/damage pair; damage is addressed through `Damageable`, so the same hit path can affect enemies, damaged towers and intercepted projectiles. | Basic projectile-hit and generic damage systems still need to be written for the game. The extension is a new data component, and not a separate resolver for each type of tower; all three targets must really be `Damageable`. |
| **Frost Charge:** a hit inflicts a temporary slow or freeze. | `OnHitStatus{Chilled}` uses common apply/expiry systems. `RateScale` reads the usual enemy-progress, tower-cooldown and projectile-motion systems; the time change is expressed by a component/parameter rather than an entity type check. | This only works if the turrets and incoming projectiles are deliberately made `Statusable` and given the appropriate timing parameter. For one original enemy slower, such coverage does not arise. |

These mechanics can reuse base systems **within a future game**, but they are not ready-made Bendvy systems or properties of the current three-lane slice. You will have to implement game-specific targeting, hit/damage, status and rate behavior. Their overall interface should be designed so that the next improvements are data and components, rather than new kind branches. If adding interceptable projectiles and tower statuses just for the sake of these requests is unpleasant or unreadable, don't pretend that TD has already proven such a reuse: this hypothesis then loses to Magicka in cross-composition and FTL/Cosmoteer in connections.

A double check of competitors' sources confirms the game mechanics, not the ECS of the original games:

- The publisher's page [Magicka](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/magicka/about) confirms the combination of elements, challenge with waves and single mode. The manual referenced by [report 04](04-systemic-sandbox.md) is needed separately for precise status rules; they cannot be attributed to one page of the publisher.
- The report by the creator [Baba Is You](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/presentations/Reading%20the%20rules_Teikari_Arvi.pdf) describes system queries to active rules and the application of qualities to objects. The same report says that the release is mainly implemented in Lua with tables. This is a good counterexample to the “looks like query, so needs ECS” argument: the semantics are strong, but arrays/tables remain a plausible implementation.
- The official page [FTL](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/) explicitly lists crew orders, power distribution and weapon target selection, as well as a pause for issuing orders. This allows for richer team roles and phases than passive towers; the page does not claim that the game uses ECS.
- Developer Page [Cosmoteer](https://store.steampowered.com/app/799600/Cosmoteer_Starship_Architect_Commander/) confirms ship shape/room design, crew roles, task priorities, individual module destruction, and physical combat. This is stronger for connections and composition changes, but also directly confirms the large amount of work outside the ECS.

Solution clarification: **lane-defense is recommended as a quick, complete game cut within the tower-defense direction; for the overall goal of maximizing ECS exposure, it is not a leader until the listed common statuses/ammo and system overlaps are proven.**

## Objections and conditions of refusal

- **Arrays may well be simpler.** For 24 cells, a small number of towers and several enemies, you can store `Plant?` in a cell array, a list of enemies in rows, a projectile array and a short update. The row index immediately selects the target, and the cell dictionary/table simplifies placement. If all slicer elements have a fixed number of views and constant fields, such code may be clearer than ECS.
- **ECS is only useful if the composition is actually used.** Practical reason to keep ECS: one `Position/Velocity` serves different families; statuses are applied based on the presence of components; two improvement branches change the available systems through the composition; declared queries express read/write scope; phases/barriers control the birth and removal of entities. If most calls still go through the general list and `kind`-switch, select arrays.
- **The path should not hide the ECS payoff.** For a PvZ-like slice, moving along a fixed row is simple arithmetic. For a Bloons-like map, you can use a custom polyline route and store the segment/progress. Dungeon Defenders already pushes for full movement/navigation, encounters, and a hero-controlled stage; ECS does not supply these subsystems. If you need A*, navmesh, dynamic obstacles or 3D physics, discard DD as the first cut.
- **The improvement graph should not become a hidden solver.** The static DAG is the content. If an interesting game requires a large unlock tree, a store, a persistent profile and balancing dozens of branches, the verifiable reason to use ECS will disappear at first. Leave a couple of options that change the composition of the tower; Otherwise, reconsider the volume.
- **Cutting should not erase the game.** Without a limited resource, row/building choice, advancing waves and losing, all you get is a display of shells. Removing one of these elements means that the clone no longer short-circuits the player's decision.
- **Events and relationships should solve real problems.** If one reader is simply forwarding data in one big update, multiple readers are decorative. If the link `FiredBy` is not used for authorship/cleaning/observation, it can be deleted. Don’t turn every cell, wave or offer into an entity for show.
- **Do not claim speed.** A small fixed world may be faster and clearer with arrays; neither game pages nor this model compare Bendvy and array performance. A separate equal workload and measurement is required if speed is ever an issue.

Reject the recommendation of the first cut if a simple array model provides the same clarity and isolation of logic, and the ECS option is forced to keep the `kind`-switch; if position/status/shots are not reused between families; if complex navigation/content is needed for recognition; or if the protected game does not provide a meaningful choice over an obvious win/lose. Reject *Dungeon Defenders* as first target if hero/action and 3D host exceed ECS issue cost. Save TD as a genre if local research confirms that a small grid-counterpart exhibits composition and scheduling with less host volume than other candidates.
