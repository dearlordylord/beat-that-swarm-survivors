# 28 — matched one-block mission: Streets of Rogue vs station and colony

**Question.** Does a one-block *Streets of Rogue* (SoR)-like mission make a strong single-player alternative to the station and colony interaction slices in [24a](24a-station-matched-interaction.md) and [24b](24b-colony-matched-interaction.md), once we count non-ECS solvers fairly and account for Bendvy's current actor–item–target access path?

**Conclusion.** SoR is a strong *product-shape* alternative if the goal is a solo systemic mission with multiple player-authored solutions. The same barrier and objective can be reached by hacking a lock or by incapacitating its guard and using the guard's access card; both routes reuse actor state, item ownership, lock state, movement and mission evaluation. Those two route rules are an original slice design, not claims about the shipped game's exact level or item logic. This comparison does **not** make SoR the strongest current-Bendvy ECS/API exercise: the signature item-to-other-entity interaction exposes the ordinary API's target-join and multi-family commit gaps, and a recognizable SoR slice brings NPC perception/intent and a dart hit solver. SS14 remains the strongest documented ECS reference ([official ECS guide](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html)); SoR is a credible solo alternative to a multiplayer-centered station clone, not a solver-adjusted query-count winner.

No game, implementation, test, or benchmark was run. All component and query names below are a proposed Bendvy consumer model. They do not describe the commercial games' internal architectures.

## Matched mission and evidence limits

The normalized kernel is the same for each design: one actor has the scarce input in hand, stands at a blocked passage, changes the barrier state, crosses the now-usable route, and reaches a concrete goal. Keep the map authored and tiny; there is no procedural generation in this comparison. The differences in how the item reaches the actor and how the barrier is changed remain visible:

| Candidate | Scarce input and barrier action | Goal and consequence | What is source-backed; what is adapted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SoR-like block** | The player begins with either one cyberdeck charge or one tranquilizer dart. A locked barrier can be hacked, or the dart can incapacitate its guard so the player can take the guard's access card and open the same barrier. | Take the mission parcel from the newly accessible room and reach the exit. The parcel/exit mission and both barrier routes are this proposed block. | SoR's developer describes mission goals solved using character traits, items, and the environment; calls out hacking computers, a doctor using chloroform/tranquilizer darts, and a bartender talking past guards. The particular cyberdeck charge, hackable barrier, keycard, guard placement, parcel, and exit are invented content. The source supports the design space, not these exact mechanics. |
| **SS14 station, 24a** | The actor starts adjacent with a partly fueled welder and repairs a damaged barrier. | Cross the changed hazard boundary and reach the evacuation control. | SS14 source contains tool-quality/fuel-gated repair, a window-repair integration test, tool-driven weld state, and a separate atmosphere exposure path. The exact composite “repairable breach becomes safe passage” is explicitly a proposed fixture in 24a, not a tested stock prototype. |
| **RimWorld colony, 24b** | A pawn gets the scarce building material from a physical stockpile and carries it to a replacement-door blueprint at the damaged opening; it is a replacement-build adaptation, not a claim that native door repair consumes material. | Restore a route to the meal stockpile before hunger/cold become critical. | Ludeon documents physical stockpiles, filters/priorities and hauling; its developer clarifies that prioritizing a blueprint can queue the material-hauling job rather than an end-to-end build. The replacement door, fixed storm hazard and thresholds are proposed. The upstream job/haul phase is retained as a colony-specific extension beyond the normalized carried-input kernel. |

The SoR official page supports single-player, procedural city missions, player choice through traits/items/environment, AI-controlled denizens and the listed stealth/hacking examples. It does not document an ECS, component layout, or the cost/implementation of its AI. [Matt Dabrowski, *Streets of Rogue* developer page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/512900/). The SS14 and Ludeon source boundaries are recorded in [24a](24a-station-matched-interaction.md) and [24b](24b-colony-matched-interaction.md), respectively.

The three barriers are not semantically identical: SS14's baseline repairs damage, the colony baseline builds a replacement, and the SoR prototype bypasses a lock. They share the player-visible causal shape—scarce input changes a barrier, and the barrier changes access to the goal—while retaining each game's characteristic action. Do not treat “one query per internal solver stage” as ECS breadth or infer source architecture from this surface resemblance.

## Two SoR routes through shared capabilities

These are two alternatives for one objective, not two scripted branches in a universal `ActionKind` resolver:

1. **Hack the barrier.** The player uses a carried single-charge deck on an electronic lock. A `Hackable` target progresses to an unlocked `LockState`; the door's normal passage/movement behavior then admits the player.
2. **Sedate and borrow access.** The player fires a carried tranquilizer dart at the guard. A shared actor-status transition incapacitates that guard; the player transfers the guard's access card into their own inventory and uses the same lock/opening behavior as route 1.

Both strategies require a `PlayerControl` actor with position/status, item ownership, a target lock state, the same movement and barrier state, and the same parcel/exit evaluator. The routes diverge at cohesive capabilities that genuinely differ: hack progress on a `Hackable` barrier versus projectile delivery, status on an `Actor`, and credential transfer. The second route must not be implemented as a special “if actor is guard, drop key” escape hatch; an incapacitated actor remains a member of the same `ActorStatus` model, while the credential is an ordinary transferable item. An enum or local branch inside a focused hacking/weapon/lock system is fine. The granularity failure would be one global handler taking ownership of unrelated input, AI, projectile, inventory, status, lock, movement and objective rules.

One possible data composition—not a one-marker-per-field prescription—is:

| Family | Example state | Independent consumers |
|---|---|---|
| Player and guard | Shared `Position`, `Health`, cohesive `ActorStatus`, `Faction`; player adds `PlayerControl + Inventory`; guard adds `AIIntent + Awareness + Inventory` | Movement and damage/status can operate on both actors. Input selects player-controlled actors; perception/intent selects AI actors. Both read the shared status state (or optional `Unconscious` capability) so unconsciousness has a common consequence. |
| Barrier | `Position`, `BarrierState{closed/open}`, cohesive `LockState`; optional `Hackable{progress, difficulty}` | Hacking selects targets with `Hackable`; lock/opening and movement select the same barrier state. A field state can change in place; a membership change is not required just to claim ECS composition. |
| Deck, dart, card, parcel | `Item` and ownership; deck has charge/hack capability, dart has projectile/status payload, card has credential data, parcel has objective data | Inventory/transfer, hacking eligibility, projectile delivery, and objective possession select different useful capabilities. A held-by relation supports transfer/cleanup, not magical access to the held entity's row. |
| Mission | Small resource with alarm/parcel/exit outcome | Goal evaluation and distinct alert/log/HUD readers consume committed facts. Event readers are not additional entity queries. |

Use a common `ActorStatus` value (for example, `unconscious_until`) or an optional `Unconscious` capability if status expiry and several consumers have a meaningful independent membership need. Either is a valid design. Do not equate structural insertion with every runtime behavior change. The key compositional test is that independent control, AI, status, inventory, lock, and objective consumers select the data they own, not that every state field gets its own component.

## Query ledger and specialized solver boundaries

Here “independent query” means a registered family selection with its own read/write responsibility. A validation routine over actor, item and target handles is an interaction planner, not three extra queries. A relation traversal, a helper, each tile visited by a solver, or a second reader of one event is likewise not added to the query count.

| Proposed consumer | Read set | Write set | Boundary / comparison note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Q_player_control` | Player actor `PlayerControl + ActorStatus + Position` | Player intent | Separates direct input from NPC decision. It does not write the target barrier. |
| `Q_npc_intent` | Guard `AIIntent + Awareness + ActorStatus + Position`; local visibility/alert result | Guard intent | This is a distinct actor consumer. Perception geometry/visibility and path selection are service/solver work, not extra ECS queries. A status change can suppress or alter intent without a guard-kind branch in the status system. |
| `Q_hackable_barrier` | Current barrier `Hackable + LockState + BarrierState`; validated action plan includes actor/deck handles | Hack progress and then the existing lock/barrier fields | Target-family consumer. The source page supports hacking as a play option, not this exact capability or implementation. Keep hack local to targets that own `Hackable`; don't let it become the universal interaction resolver. |
| `Q_projectile` / hit planning | Projectile `Position + Velocity + Lifetime` and spatial collision result | Projectile movement/lifetime; emits a planned hit result | Specialized projectile/collision boundary. The later actor-family pass applies the status to its current row; the projectile callback does not gain a foreign-row writer. |
| `Q_actor_status` | Current actor `ActorStatus` and planned hit/status changes | Current actor `ActorStatus` / health | Shared by player and guard actors. Independent status expiry can be a second consumer only if it owns a distinct timed rule; don't split it merely to inflate counts. |
| `Q_lock_use` | Current barrier lock and a validated credential/hack result | Existing `LockState + BarrierState` | Both strategies converge here. Credential lookup/ownership is supplied by an explicit plan; the target row doesn't dynamically join into inventory. |
| `Q_item_transfer` | Current card/parcel item and planned owner change | Item ownership relation/state, normally at an explicit structural barrier | Separate item-family lifecycle. Ownership is a relation where transfer/inverse lookup and cleanup justify it. |
| `Q_mission_goal` | Parcel possession, player alive/at-exit, committed barrier/action outcome | Mission result | A distinct reusable objective consumer. `AlarmRaised`, if included, can independently inform AI, mission and HUD/log readers, but those event readers are not queries. |
| `Q_station_tool` / `Q_station_repair_target` | Tool quality/fuel and current `Repairable + Damageable + BarrierState` target | Fuel and target damage/barrier value in their respective family passes | From 24a's proposed model. SS14 source backs tool and repair mechanics; the tested repair does not establish hazard closure. |
| `Q_station_exposure` / `Q_station_motion` / `Q_station_goal` | Exposed actor state plus atmosphere result; actor position/barrier passability; evacuation goal | Actor consequence; actor position; outcome | Actor exposure is a distinct ECS-facing consumer after one atmosphere solver update. The one-step matched goal needs local movement/collision, not a navmesh. |
| `Q_colony_work` / `Q_material` / `Q_door` | Pawn job/work state; material kind/count/position; current door/build state | Work progress; material quantity/carry stage; replacement-door state | From 24b's proposed model. Job selection/reservation is one specialized scheduling pass, not a pile of separately counted queries. |
| `Q_colony_needs` / `Q_colony_motion` / `Q_colony_goal` | Pawn needs and climate result; pawn/door route state; shelter/meal goal | Need/health consequence; pawn position; outcome | Separate entity consumers integrate the environment/path results. The material haul and post-build travel add path requests that the fixed adjacent SS14 kernel does not have. |

The independent ECS surface is real in the *proposed consumer* if those are implemented as separately owned consumers and data is shared across both actor modes and both solution paths. The concrete one-interaction case alone does not prove that decomposition is clearer than a direct resolver or plain arrays. If the small build leaves all decisions in one `interact(actor,item,target)` routine and only uses ECS for storage, the ECS case has failed its own evaluation criterion.

Count solver/domain work once per boundary and report its inputs/consumers separately:

| Candidate | Solver/service work the matched slice actually needs | Fairness check |
|---|---|---|
| **SoR** | Fixed authored tile map; movement and collision; interaction range/visibility; guard perception/intent and any short guard route; projectile trajectory/hit collision for the sedation route; hack progress/time. | Do not charge the full game's procedural city generator to a fixed one-block prototype. Do count the guard policy/perception and projectile collision required by the chosen sedation route. Raycasts, path cells, and projectile substeps are internals of those boundaries, not ECS query multipliers. If the block keeps an active guard for both routes, NPC AI is common slice cost. |
| **SS14 24a** | One local atmosphere/grid update after the proposed barrier state change; actor exposure selection/effect; welder fuel-solution lookup/consumption; timed tool action; adjacency/range and movement collision. | Count the atmosphere network/mixture traversal once. The pinned source does not show that the repaired test window changes airtight state. No atmosphere substage is another ECS query; no pathfinder is required for the matched one-step crossing. |
| **Colony 24b** | One job-selection/reservation routine, pathfinding for the stockpile-to-blueprint haul and subsequent access, material travel/carry, construction progress, one local room/climate update and pawn need update. | Its job/haul and climate work are native to the proposed colony trace; they cannot be charged as ECS breadth. Conversely, don't add “AI cost” to SoR but omit colony job choice/pathfinding, or count every climate cell as a query. In the normalized carried-at-target kernel the haul phase is absent; retain it only when scoring the fuller colony loop. |

All three still need a host for input, presentation, map collision/movement and goal feedback. SoR's active guard and dart add solver work beyond the minimal station action; SS14's atmosphere and the proposed colony climate/job systems add their own work. These are unlike boundaries and cannot be meaningfully converted into a common numeric “solver count.” The fair comparison is a disclosed work inventory, then an explicit choice of product goal—not pretending the most complicated candidate's internals are more independent ECS queries.

## Bendvy fit: phase and target joins are the deciding risk

The important SoR action is inherently cross-entity: a player uses a held deck/card/dart on a barrier or guard. In the current ordinary App route, a component writer addresses the current row; relation views can return detached handles but are read-only; an explicit other-handle write is deferred and becomes visible at a barrier. The ordinary relation path does not provide the convenience composition “actor → held item → read item components → target components → journal-write both target and item in one transaction.” See the corrected [cross-entity audit 12](12-cross-entity-access-audit.md).

This is an ergonomic/API constraint rather than an absolute statement that the game cannot be implemented. The audit permits an immutable interaction/hit plan followed by typed family passes, deferred commands with explicit barrier semantics, or a custom checked `Ops`/query adapter. It also confirms a recognized App `pair(write, resource_write)` for a current component row plus a whole resource; that useful path does not grant arbitrary outgoing-target component access. Do not claim that storing a target handle in an event or work ledger makes the target writable, or that `Handle<Schema>` being `Data` proves current-row identity is conveniently bound in every ordinary callback.

For the prototype, keep `LockState`, `BarrierState`, and `ActorStatus` as values on existing rows unless optional membership has an independent consumer. Validate actor/item/target liveness and eligibility, build a typed plan, and apply item, actor, and target updates through explicitly ordered passes. If a status/hack effect crosses a structural barrier, treat it as a staged action: the late barrier is not transactionally rolled back with already committed writes, so do not publish `BarrierOpened` or `MissionWon` until the target state is visible and validated. Independent alert/AI/UI readers receive committed facts; the action's accept/refuse result stays synchronous rather than becoming an event that cannot veto the cost.

This is a sharper API stress test than the 24a/24b target-local barrier updates, but it's also the largest obstacle to getting a first playable SoR slice cleanly through the current standard route. SS14 itself has synchronous tool/user/target callbacks and timed use; that behavior must not be mapped onto Bendvy `Data` events as if the transaction semantics were the same. A prototype should either verify a custom composite action adapter before choosing SoR as the API showcase, or explicitly accept staged/reconciliation semantics. If the task is instead to validate Bendvy's ordinary query path with minimum integration work, a same-family push/hit simulation is a safer first step.

## Decision under the matched comparison

SoR does justify a strong solo alternative **for the game-design objective**: unlike a station clone whose main identity is many-player role coordination, one authored mission with social/stealth/tech approaches is native to SoR's described mission format. The two routes create a visible need for reusable actor state, ownership/transfer, target capabilities, independent control/AI, and post-commit objective readers. This is more player-facing composition than the single repair/build route in 24a or 24b.

It does not justify the stronger claim that SoR has the most ECS leverage per unit of implementation or that its query count beats those candidates. The station slice has direct source evidence for actual component-system boundaries and synchronous tool flow, though its proposed passage/hazard composition is unverified. The colony slice preserves stockpile, hauling, job and needs choices, but its replacement-door consequence is an adaptation and it pays a scheduler/path/climate budget. SoR offers more alternative behaviors in the same block, but must pay guard AI/perception and (for the sedative route) projectile collision, then confronts Bendvy's actor–item–target join gap. Under an equalized fixed block, those costs remain visible and none is converted into “extra queries.”

**Recommendation:** retain SoR as the strongest solo systemic-mission alternative, not as a replacement for SS14's lead in documented ECS relevance. Pick it if the desired artifact is a small, choice-rich single-player mission and the team accepts a bespoke interaction phase or stages the action honestly. Pick the station slice if source-backed ECS/tool/environment integration matters more than preserving a single-player core. Pick the colony slice if work priority, material logistics, and needs are the desired systems, with the understanding that the matched material-replacement rule is custom. If no independent consumers survive the scope cut, use a plain-array/action resolver; the mission alone does not prove ECS superiority.
