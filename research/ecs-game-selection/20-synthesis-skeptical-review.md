# Skeptical review of the provisional synthesis

**Scope.** This review challenges the SS14-first claim in [the provisional `REPORT.md`](REPORT.md); it does not amend that report or its source studies. I read the evaluation contract, the provisional synthesis, the SS14 and colony studies, the modular-ship and rule-puzzle studies, and the cross-reviews and API audit. I also checked the primary SS14, RimWorld, FTL, Baba Is You, and Streets of Rogue sources linked below. No code, game, test, or benchmark was run.

## Verdict

SS14 is a credible provisional leader for **the breadth of ECS-integrated mechanics in a shared station world**. The research shows real reusable seams in tools, damage, containment, power, atmosphere, and round flow, and SS14 is unusually well documented at the ECS level. That is a useful basis for an ECS-oriented reference project.

The stronger statement that SS14 is *the* game that maximizes ECS is not established yet. The synthesis counts SS14's environment and power mechanics as evidence of cross-system breadth, but uses a hypothetical global job allocator and pathfinder to demote RimWorld. That comparison needs one consistent rule for deciding whether a domain-level solver counts as ECS work, ECS integration work, or work outside ECS. The evaluation contract names both independent query surfaces and specialized solver burden, but neither the contract nor report says how those dimensions decide the overall winner. [The report itself marks the scores as qualitative and its rank as provisional.](17-independent-ranking.md#отдельные-оси)

I would keep SS14 as a **conditional breadth candidate**, and separate that from the best game to finish on current Bendvy. The current evidence supports three different strengths: SS14 for broad ECS integration, *Baba Is You* for visible runtime composition changes, and *Streets of Rogue* or FTL for a bounded single-player or relational slice. It does not support one unconditional winner across all three purposes.

## What survives the skeptical pass

The architecture claim for SS14 is unusually strong. Its official [ECS guide](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html) describes data/marker components, behavior in entity systems, and event communication. The pinned [`DestructibleSystem`](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Destructible/DestructibleSystem.cs) connects damage thresholds to atmosphere, construction, explosions, containers, solutions, stacks, and triggers. That is direct evidence of an ECS-integrated cross-system seam, not merely a marketing description.

It also shows why “more named subsystems” cannot be equated with “more independent component queries.” The pinned [`PowerNetSystem`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Power/EntitySystems/PowerNetSystem.cs) handles component initialization/removal and queries power receivers, but also keeps a system-owned `PowerState`, reconnects topology, and calls a dedicated solver each update. The pinned [`AtmosphereSystem`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Atmos/EntitySystems/AtmosphereSystem.cs) combines tile/map state, node groups, physics, containers, and lookup; its entity-query boundary applies exposure effects to entities. These are strong examples of **ECS integrated with specialized solvers**, not proof that atmosphere flow and power distribution are ordinary orthogonal ECS queries. Calling them wholly “outside ECS” would also be wrong: both are entity systems with component-driven lifecycle and effects.

The game-identity caveat is also correct. The official [SS14 description](https://spacestation14.com/about/about/) calls it a round-based multiplayer role-playing game and makes player roles and emergent social situations central. A solo engineer emergency may be a useful station-inspired prototype, but it is not a faithful small SS14 clone. The report already recognizes this distinction; its top recommendation should preserve the word **reference** or **stress test** if the chosen slice omits multiplayer social play.

## The main comparative gap: solvers are treated asymmetrically

The synthesis calls RimWorld's job allocation and pathfinding a reason its component richness may be hidden behind a central planner. That is a legitimate implementation risk, but the report has no RimWorld core-source audit. The cited Ludeon [Update 1.6 announcement](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/) says late-game performance motivated batching and multithreading the pathfinding system. It does **not** establish that RimWorld's job selection is one monolithic allocator, that needs/social/health/hauling/production are not independently organized, or that a small colony slice needs production-scale pathfinding. The report should label the allocator argument as a falsifiable modeling hypothesis, not an observed architecture or decisive penalty.

Meanwhile, SS14's power and atmosphere solvers are listed as high cost outside ECS, but the breadth argument still counts them among the same entities' many ECS interactions. That can be coherent if the measure is **how many game domains the ECS must integrate**. It is not coherent if the measure is **how much of those domains is expressed as independently composed entity queries**. State which definition applies, then apply it to every candidate:

| Candidate | ECS-shaped surface supported by the current record | Solver/centralization uncertainty that must be treated symmetrically |
|---|---|---|
| SS14 | Tools, damage, containment, lifecycle events, and round outcome have source-backed system seams. | Atmosphere is substantially grid/map/network state; power is a component-facing network solver with system-owned state. Multiplayer and rendering/network host add further work. Count these as ECS-integrated domains, not as free query breadth. |
| RimWorld | Needs, health, relations, inventory, hauling, crafting, and jobs expose a broad set of candidate components and interactions. | Work assignment and reachability may be central/global, but the report has no core source showing where the boundaries actually lie. A pathfinding optimization is not evidence that the job loop is one global switch. |
| Cosmoteer | Modular build/destruction, crew tasks, targeted modules, fire, and repair create strong structural and relation churn. | Connectivity, crew pathing, physics, and editor geometry can be specialized global steps. This is a real cost, but it should be compared with SS14's topology solvers on the same axis. |
| Streets of Rogue | Player/NPC/prop interactions, status, items, and several mission approaches offer multiple cross-family behaviors. | A general interaction resolver and NPC perception could centralize behavior; this is a risk in the proposed implementation, not a source-backed description of its architecture. |
| FTL | Crew–room–system–order links, power choices, repair/hazards, pause, and run phases form a compact relational simulation. | Fixed room layouts reduce structural composition; crew routing and encounter decisions remain specialized concerns. Its narrower breadth is clear, but it may offer better ECS signal per host-system built. |
| Baba Is You | Rules literally add/remove capabilities used by movement, blocking, control, and victory systems; creator materials describe rule-oriented system queries. | The room has few entity families and almost no lasting relation graph. Its high score is specifically dynamic composition, not breadth across a large simulation. |

This table is deliberately qualitative. The missing decision rule is whether one point of “special solver burden” cancels any of the game's additional mechanic breadth. The evaluation contract lists that burden but says not to average the axes into a pseudo-precise score. As written, “SS14 wins by breadth” is plausible only if the intended objective is **count integrated gameplay domains regardless of implementation share**. If “maximum ECS” means more independent, reusable component queries relative to global solver work, the winner is undetermined.

## Evidence confidence is not Bendvy suitability

SS14's docs and pinned source increase confidence that the proposed system boundaries occur in a real game. They do not show that reproducing those boundaries is the most useful test of the current Bendvy API. The [cross-entity audit](12-cross-entity-access-audit.md) finds that an ordinary writer mutates the current row; a relation query returns detached handles without target-component access; and arbitrary actor → item/tool → target updates need a phase, typed plan, deferred command, or custom adapter. That is especially material for the SS14 slice's defining tool-on-door/machine interactions. The audit calls this expressible, but less direct and not one atomic multi-target commit.

There are two defensible intents, and they imply different selections:

1. If this is a **Bendvy stress test intended to reveal API gaps**, SS14's troublesome actor/tool/target interaction is a reason to choose it. The cost is that the work may primarily test adapters, network/grid host, and specialized solvers before it tests how pleasant the existing ECS is.
2. If this is a **finished game consumer on today's ordinary App route**, SS14's cross-entity writes and multiplayer identity are liabilities, not benefits. A narrower FTL, Baba, Magicka, or carefully bounded SoR consumer is a fairer first proof of what Bendvy already expresses. The report says this in part; it should not let the “maximum potential” headline blur the distinction.

Do not let native ECS source confidence silently become product suitability. SS14's original implementation can demonstrate patterns and correct the factual model. It does not remove the architectural translation between Robust's immediate/cancellable interaction events and Bendvy's buffered events, deferred structural barriers, and current-row writes.

## Strongest alternative arguments

**RimWorld is the strongest breadth counterargument, not a proven winner.** Its official and developer-authored sources support many distinct state domains—needs, health, social relations, physical stockpiles, hauling, production, combat, and incidents. A work scheduler is not inherently non-ECS: one or more registered systems can resolve assignments, reserve resources, and emit orders while other component queries handle needs, health, hauling, and consequences. Until that model is tested against the actual game trace, using a supposed central allocator as the reason to rank RimWorld below SS14 is speculative. Conversely, the report has more source visibility for SS14, so its apparent system breadth may simply be more inspectable.

**Cosmoteer is the strongest structural-composition counterargument.** Building and losing modules changes the ship's entity/relation graph while crew, tasks, power, fires, and targeted damage cross the same space. Its graph/physics/editor costs are formidable and may dominate a small clone, but that is a reason to lower first-clone suitability—not automatically a reason to exclude it from a maximum-composition contest.

**Streets of Rogue is the strongest single-player mission alternative.** Its developer's [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/512900/Streets_of_Rogue/) describes mission goals that can be approached through character traits, items, and the environment. This makes a one-block mission a plausible recognizable slice with actor–item–prop–NPC interactions and multiple routes. The one-block model still needs real alternatives and independent systems; otherwise a single `interact(target, action)` switch and AI resolver erase the ECS claim. The current Bendvy cross-entity limitation is a concrete implementation risk here too.

**FTL is the strongest bounded relational/phase alternative.** Subset Games' [official page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/) explicitly lists crew orders, power distribution, target choice, pausing, and a randomized galaxy. The smaller fixed-layout ship has less structural churn than a station or Cosmoteer, but it has a clear crew-to-room/system assignment loop and authored multi-encounter run. It may demonstrate relations and ordered simulation with less custom spatial infrastructure; it does not beat Baba on runtime trait changes.

**Baba Is You is the strongest dynamic-composition alternative.** Creator Arvi Teikari's [GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/presentations/Reading%20the%20rules_Teikari_Arvi.pdf) say gameplay systems ask which rules are present and which objects they apply to. This validates a direct composition demonstration, not an original ECS implementation. It is the cleaner choice if the desired lesson is “one player action changes which entities participate in which behaviors.” Its small family/relationship surface makes it a weak counter-winner for breadth across many game domains.

## Corrections needed before freezing a winner

1. **Define the winning axis in the recommendation.** Suggested wording: “SS14 is the provisional leader if maximum means the number of cross-family mechanics integrated through ECS systems, including systems backed by dedicated grid/network solvers. It is not yet shown to lead on the fraction of gameplay expressed as independent compositional queries, nor is it the best current-Bendvy clone.”
2. **Make the solver rule symmetric.** Keep job allocation, reachability, atmosphere, power topology, AI perception, connectivity, and interaction resolution in a separate column. Treat them all as solver risk until code or a traced implementation shows which are ECS-native, ECS-integrated, or external.
3. **Downgrade the RimWorld planner statement.** Say that job allocation/pathing could dominate the small colony loop and needs a trace or source audit. The 1.6 source supports a late-game pathfinding optimization, not a monolithic job-allocation claim.
4. **Label invented component overlap as design, not existing content.** For example, an object that is simultaneously a tool, container, and hazard is a useful proposed ECS test; do not imply that this exact composition is a common or source-confirmed SS14 entity unless a concrete prototype/source demonstrates it.
5. **Preserve the prototype/clone distinction in the headline.** A solo station emergency is not the same product as the official multiplayer role-play round. Keep SS14 as the source-backed breadth/stress-test option; state a separate current-API first-clone choice.

Before changing “provisional” to “winner,” compare a matched trace for SS14, RimWorld or Cosmoteer, and one compact alternative. For each trace, list entity families, components read/written, independent queries/systems, structural changes/relations, and solver calls. Count integrated domain solvers once as integration boundaries, not again as evidence that every query is independent. This need not be a benchmark; it is the minimum evidence needed to support a comparative architecture judgment. If the team does not want that additional work, leave the ranking explicitly conditional.

## Source checks and limits

Primary claims inspected for this review:

- Space Wizards, [SS14 official ECS guide](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html), [official About](https://spacestation14.com/about/about/), and pinned [AtmosphereSystem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Atmos/EntitySystems/AtmosphereSystem.cs), [PowerNetSystem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Power/EntitySystems/PowerNetSystem.cs), and [DestructibleSystem](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Destructible/DestructibleSystem.cs).
- Ludeon, [Update 1.6 announcement](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/) for the scope of the pathfinding optimization.
- Subset Games, [FTL on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/); Matt Dabrowski / tinyBuild, [Streets of Rogue on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/512900/Streets_of_Rogue/); Arvi Teikari, [Baba Is You GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/presentations/Reading%20the%20rules_Teikari_Arvi.pdf).

These sources verify gameplay claims and selected SS14 architecture. They do not reveal RimWorld, FTL, SoR, or Baba internal architecture; the component/system maps for those games remain design hypotheses. This review is an analytical challenge, not a replacement ranking or an executed game comparison.
