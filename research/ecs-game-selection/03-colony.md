# Hypothesis 3: RimWorld / Dwarf Fortress / Oxygen Not Included

Status: initial hypothesis report, 2026-10-21. This is not the final game choice for the entire study, nor is it a specification for implementation. I am comparing two different goals according to [general evaluation contract](00-evaluation-contract.md): maximum ECS breadth and the best first completed clone. Sources about the mechanics below are the official pages of the developers and their materials; the breakdown into components and systems is our design hypothesis, not a statement about the design of the original engines.

## Conclusion on this hypothesis

If the priority is **maximum breadth of independent gameplay mechanics**, Dwarf Fortress wins within this trio: colony life connects work, needs, relations, crafts, conflict, injuries, weather, a multilevel map and world history. Such richness provides many ECS surfaces, but a full clone is practically unlimited in content and systems.

If the priority is the **best first recognizable clone**, the preliminary choice is a RimWorld-like colony. The game has a dense mixture of changing pawns, work and supplies, psychology, relationships, ecology and threats, and its slice can be limited to one 2D map and one game episode. This is enough to show the composition and interaction of systems without claiming the entire simulator. Main objection: Job scheduler and pathfinding may become a bigger part of the product than ECS.

Oxygen Not Included is a strong alternative choice if the goal is to show interdependent environments and control systems. But gas, liquids, heat, electricity and pipes make the dense cellular simulation the centerpiece of the game. These fields should not be automatically modeled with a per-cell entity; we need a hybrid of ECS and specialized grid/field storage.

## What primary sources confirm

**RimWorld.** The official page describes the history of the colony through psychology, ecology, gunfire and melee combat, climate, biomes, diplomacy, interpersonal relationships, art, medicine and trade ([official game page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/)). Ludeon separately described family ties, pairings that came and went, and characters' changing opinions of each other ([devlog about social relationships ](https://ludeon.com/blog/2016/01/progress-continues/)). In an earlier devlog, the developer described storing items physically on the map, stockpiling zones with filters and priorities, as well as the work of carriers between zones ([Alpha 1: stockpiling](https://ludeon.com/blog/2014/01/alpha-1-released/)). This is a good basis for the cycle of "need → work order → resource search → transfer/production → effect on colonist."

Ludeon also described a narrator who introduces travelers and hostile events, and the player can capture, recruit, ignore or attack the people who arrive ([devlog about choosing a narrator](https://ludeon.com/blog/2013/09/title-screen-update/)). In update 1.6, the developers announced that pathfinding was being reworked into a batch multi-threaded system and that the load of late colonies was distributed across multiple systems ([announcement 1.6](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/)). In an early technical devlog, Ludeon specifically addressed the failure of pathfinding and the addition of flood-fill markers of reachable areas on top of the map ([reachability](https://ludeon.com/blog/2013/07/reachability-at-last/)). This is a clear warning against assuming that the ECS query will decide the pathfinding and scaling itself.

**Dwarf Fortress.** Bay 12 describes a persistent procedural world with history and many civilizations, and in the fortress - crafts, defense, trade, the requirements of the nobility, the thoughts and needs of the dwarves, healing, Z levels, water and magma, social institutions, weather, animals and hundreds of raw materials ([official list of features ](https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/features.html)). The development log shows the intersection zones of several areas assigned to workers and the restrictions on their use of workshops, warehouses and premises ([devlog about burrows](https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2009.html)). Another official log describes the posting of orders, priorities, temporary removal of workers from less important jobs, and the independent selection of tasks by dwarves ([jobs and priorities, 2014](https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/dev_2014.html)). This is particularly powerful material for comparing relationships, job scheduling and life cycles.

**Oxygen Not Included.** Klei positions the game around the lack of oxygen, heat and food ([official page of Klei](https://www.klei.com/games/oxygen-not-included)). The official Steam page details simulations of stress, temperature, gases and liquids, pipes, electrical networks and waste recycling ([game page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/457140/Oxygen_Not_Included/)). The Spaced Out add-on expands the cycle to multiple planetoids and the transfer of resources between bases ([description Klei](https://www.klei.com/games/oxygen-not-included)). These are many individual systems with clearly visible causal connections, but they largely operate on dense fields and connected networks.

### Boundary between evidence and code

Official materials confirm the game mechanics, and not the ECS architecture of the games. They found no reason to attribute ECS, archetype storage, or a specific component model to these games. Dwarf Fortress since 2025 has its own Lua scripts and examples of vanilla scripts for generating individual procedural objects; it is an accessible content surface, but not an open implementation of the entire simulation ([Bay 12 announcement on the official ](https://www.bay12games.com/dwarves/index.html) page). Among the public repositories of the official Ludeon GitHub organization that I looked at were localizations, not the core gameplay source ([Ludeon on GitHub](https://github.com/Ludeon)). The full ONI code was not found or analyzed in this study. Therefore, the code source is not sufficient to infer the internal ECS architecture of the candidates; design analysis and public mechanics were used for ranking.

## How this might fit on ECS

This decomposition is a **proposal for the prototype**, not a reproduction of the internal types of any of the three games.

| Game object | Possible data composition | Overlapping game requests |
|---|---|---|
| Settler, captive, guest, raider or animal | `Position`, `Needs`, `Health`, `Skills`, `WorkPolicy`, `Inventory`, optional `Hostile`, `Captive`, `CanHaul`, `CanTreat`, `CanFight` | Needs, work planning, movement, combat, healing, mood and target selection work with common basic components and different combinations of abilities |
| Item or resource stack | `Position`, `Material`, `Quantity`, optional `Nutrition`, `Spoilage`, `Carryable`, `Equipable` | Systems for resource availability, food, transportation, production, equipment, trade and cleaning |
| plant | `Position`, `Growth`, `Harvestable`, `EnvironmentRange`, optional `Edible`, `SeedSource` | Growth depends on the world; the collection appears at maturity; food becomes a transport/cooking task |
| Construction | `Footprint`, `Position`, plus functional parts `Storage`, `Bed`, `Workstation`, `Door`, `PowerConsumer`, `Medical` | Construction, use, room availability, production, protection and maintenance |
| Work/order | `WorkKind`, `Target`, `RequiredSkill`, `Inputs`, `Progress`, `Priority`, reserve status | Selecting works, checking materials, assigning an artist, canceling when the goal disappears, completing and publishing the result |

For real relationships, we need links, not just duplicating IDs in large components: `AssignedTo(job,pawn)`, `Targets(job,entity)`, `Carries(pawn,item)`, `StoredIn(item,stockpile)`, `OwnsBed(pawn,bed)`, `Knows(pawn,pawn)`/`RelatedTo(pawn,pawn)`. When a job dies, is lost, or is canceled, the links should be cleared or become explicitly inactive. This is a good exercise for forwarded and backward queries and cleanup semantics.

The most indicative systems: reduction of needs; creating urgent tasks; coordination of work priorities and skills; employee/item reservation; navigation; labor performance and transfer; plant growth/harvesting; production; food consumption; health/mood; processing arrivals and threats; fight; completion of work and cleanup of destroyed entities. Different subscribers may respond to `JobCompleted`, `MealEaten`, `PawnInjured`, `PawnDied`, `RaidArrived` and `RelationChanged`. It is important that these are business transition events and not repeated notifications on every tick.

Such a consumer uses ECS if one settler is actually encountered in the systems of needs, work, movement, health, relationships and combat; animal - in movement, food, threats and transfer; and new features change queries by adding/removing components. If you pack everything into one big `PawnState` and execute a single `update_colony`, a new demo will appear, but ECS will be of little use.

## Which cut to keep recognizable?

Don’t cut it down to the abstract “several actors are moving toward goals.” The proposed bounded slice is **the colony's first story after a crash**: one small map, three survivors with different skills and schedules, food scarcity, a growing plot, storage, a cooking workshop and one predeclared raid. For example, the player prioritizes cooking over transportation for the cook, but leaves the second colonist on defense: when two tasks compete for the last bag of berries, the first task reserves the item and furnace, and the second must choose another resource/job; If the player puts the cook on the defensive, food delivery will be delayed and the solution will become visible in supplies and hunger. At the beginning, the player sets priorities for work and chooses who to send to defense and who to leave for transfer/cooking; After actions, he sees actual assignments, stocks, requirements and a feed of incidents. In a short game day, you can see the assignment of work, the path and delivery of resources, the appearance of food, a drop in hunger/mood, threat, injury or death, and the final state of the colony. The success of this limited mission is to survive the raid and cook food before nightfall; defeat - all three died/kicked out of the game or night came without cooked food. This is a criterion for one scenario, not a statement about the win condition of the original RimWorld. Raiding and losing should not be reduced to a simple `damage` from an already existing arena: the player's decision and the lack of resources should change the outcome.

The first stage can limit the combat and bring only the cycle of priorities, collection, storage, cooking and eating to a complete state; then this is a recognizable colony-management story, but it reproduces the characteristic RimWorld story generator even more poorly. To consider a game clone closed, add a short episode with an external event and real player choice. The second stage can add social relationships and consequences: for example, an injured relative arrives as a guest, and the player chooses to treat him or continue working. This scenario tests the lifecycle of relationships and events without attempting to simulate all content.

Technical framework for this slice: fixed seeded scenario, compact 2D map as a grid resource/specialized storage, and not automatically an entity per cell; settlers, items, plants, buildings, enemies and tasks are separate ECS entities. A regular App registers systems explicitly; reasonable phases: `world/environment → needs → create/assign jobs → movement → work/combat → health/mood → incident schedule → barrier → observation`. The creation of harvest items, new tasks, raiders and the removal of dead go through obvious structural barriers.

Determinism does not follow from schedule order. For reproducibility, the application itself must define a seed and store the PRNG state as an explicit resource, set a stable traversal order and tie-break in the selection of workers/targets, and schedule environmental events according to seed/tick, not according to wall-clock. The log/audio/victory message should be published after a successful system commit, since external IO is not returned by the ECS transaction. Saving the current World is also not an exact execution checkpoint: basic/graph save fails if the structural queue is not empty, and queues, event-reader cursors and state/capture systems require a separate recovery policy. Therefore, it is better to restart the first slice using the seed; Don't promise bitwise continuation in the middle of a job/event. See [ordinary App guide](../../../bendvy/docs/reference/ordinary-app.md), [SPEC](../../../bendvy/docs/SPEC.md) and [current parity index](../../../bendvy/docs/parity/README.md).

## Benefits and Limits of ECS vs. Scheduler and Grid

The strength of RimWorld-like selection is not the number of entities, but the independent data that intersect in actions: a settler's trait makes it available for combat, a skill makes it available for combat, an injury makes it available for healing and speed, a need changes the urgency of tasks; different subsystems see different combinations. Tasks can be created and deleted dynamically, and relationships store the relationship between a worker, a target, a carried item, and a room. Events and schedules naturally represent the occurrence of threats, completion of activities, outcomes, and subsequent reactions.

The most difficult part is the **work scheduler**. It matches the job mix with worker mix, skills, distance, materials, priorities, and reservations; This is a global solution, not a regular independent component-loop. First let one ordered system evaluate and reserve job/pawn/item atomically in a transaction. If another task has already claimed a bag of berries, the failed reservation is rejected within the boundaries of that system; This is not an entire tick rollback, and previously successfully completed systems retain their changes. Scheduler rereads available tasks on the next pass/tick, and the UI and log show only the committed result. Don't promise concurrency until it has been implemented and proven. The second difficult place is **pathfinding and accessibility**: the path query runs along a grid and depends on obstacles/reserves, requiring a separate spatial index, reachability zones or batch navigation. The official RimWorld history explicitly states that pathfinding had to be batched and parallelized for performance ([Ludeon, 1.6](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/)).

The cellular environment is a separate limit. It is reasonable to store humidity/temperature/gas/passability and fields on tens of thousands of neighboring cells in a dense grid or chunks; they require a specialized solver/system. ECS is responsible for actors, things, buildings, local events and position references. At ONI, this compromise is even more important: many proprietary mechanics are the propagation of fields along the grid and flows through the pipe/energy network. Neither a regular ECS request nor a declared access by itself provides a spatial index, grid solver, or throughput.

## Matches current Bendvy

Bendvy is an ECS runtime, not a game engine with a ready-made renderer, input, pathfinder, audio, networking or physics. Speca builds a system around typed schemas, declared component-access capabilities, a regular App, a sequential schedule, events, relationships, barriers, rollback and inspection; The first project consumer is a simple repeatable CPU-console script. According to the current [parity](../../../bendvy/docs/parity/README.md#immediate-frontier) map, semantic feature tickets are closed, numerical optimization has been postponed, and #64 has already prepared the requirements for a separate copy of Canonical Tower Defense. Therefore colony clone is an additional user/new game option, not a continuation of #64 automatically.

Practical restrictions for cutting:

- Use the ordinary App as the main registration point. Systems are executed in order of registration; this allows for observable phases, but does not provide automatic parallelism.
- World goes through the affine owner, the system works through declared capabilities. Game logic should not change components using side alias links; isolate all actions via query/commands.
- Repeatable queries `Args`/`Out` in the regular App are limited to `Data`; arbitrary component Type payloads are allowed via owner-preserving binding, but this complicates the storage system. For the first game, keep everyday data in `Data`, leave affine captures only where the portable owner is needed.
- Deferred spawn/despawn becomes visible on an explicit barrier. Within a tick, determine in advance whether a new entity can move/attack in the same tick.
- Rollback covers ECS-owned changes to the specified system transaction boundary; host IO and capture state are not necessarily rolled back. The UI should update from a committed observation, rather than publishing the result during a potentially failed callback.
- The World snapshot does not save runtime readers, pending queues, and all system capture/queue states. The exact save/checkpoint will require a separate script and policy. Structural pending queue must be empty before basic/graph save.
- Launching on JS/Native is semantically possible through a regular Consumer, but the comparative speed of the new game slice is unknown and should remain unannounced: performance campaigns for the current SPEC are suspended.

In the current demo-browser, the arena can already be played with enemies, pickups, bolts, pulses and chains, but its model uses compound `Body` and direct query orchestration ([arena README](../../../bendvy/examples/arena/README.md)). The colony will show additional value only if the common `Pawn` is actually assembled from independent components, and the systems are registered through a regular App and use relations/events/barriers according to the meaning of the game.

## Objections and conditions under which a hypothesis can be rejected

**Could everything be implemented as an array of structs with one short update?** For a small scenario, yes. ECS is justified for a library/game only if separate systems independently process intersecting sets of pawn/item/job/building, and changing capabilities does not require rewriting a large switch in `kind`. This needs to be shown with the source consumer code and its visible state, not with the words “many entities”.

**Doesn't non-ECS complexity dominate?** Probably yes: job and pathfinding, map, reachability, user interface. If the slice turns most of the logic into grid solver and global distribution optimization, ECS may be a thin shell. In this case, ONI is a more honest grid/hybrid example, and another candidate from the general study may better highlight the ECS composition.

**Does the game remain recognizable?** The option with only the need for `Hunger`, a list of points and movement does not work. What should remain is assigning work priorities to colonists, physical gathering/transfer of resources, production or cooking, player intervention, and an external storyteller event with consequences. These elements are confirmed by the official Ludeon materials above.

**Aren't we testing APIs for the sake of APIs?** Each component must open or close an actual action; `Relation` appears because job is assigned to pawn and reserved by target, not to show the table. Transaction failure is useful when a worker, item, and station are simultaneously reserved and one resource is already occupied. The Inspector is needed to see why the pawn is idle. Snapshot is only needed for a real saving task, with a limited checkpoint scope.

**The strongest alternative is Dwarf Fortress.** If the only criterion is maximum variety of simulation, DF should be preferred: more levels of the world and history, types of entities and materials, bodies/medicine, social life, crafts and environmental simulation. If the goal is a completed first game, this benefit turns into a risk of endless expansion. Abandoning RimWorld-first in favor of DF makes sense if the client is clearly choosing a multi-year depth/simulation of the history of the world, rather than a short vertical game.

**Another type of alternative is ONI.** If the main goal is the interoperability of independent environmental management systems and resources, ONI may win. If it is expected that the entire state of the map needs to be represented by ECS entities and typical entity queries will accelerate gas/heat, this hypothesis is incorrect: the proprietary task is dense grid simulation, which means that hybrid storage should be designed in advance.

## Cross-Critique of Hypotheses 6–8: High ECS Does Not Equal Easy MVP

A review of primary sources confirmed three material statements in adjacent reports. In Space Station 14, the official ECS guide directly separates data components and behavior systems, describes events and recommends the composition of game objects; The official page of the game separately names round-based multiplayer role-play, different crew/enemy jobs and player decisions ([ECS guide](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html), [SS14 About](https://spacestation14.com/about/about/)). In FTL, publisher Subset Games describes the journey of the ship and crew through a randomly generated galaxy and a tactical pause to issue orders ([FTL, Subset Games on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/ftl_faster_than_light/)). The official press kit Caves of Qud confirms over 70 mutations, NPCs with levels, skills, equipment, factions and body parts, as well as physically destructible/meltable walls ([Qud press kit](https://cavesofqud.com/press-kit/)). These sources confirm the game's coverage; they do not by themselves prove the ECS code of either FTL, Qud, or the original RimWorld.

**Answer to the priority question: RimWorld does not win the maximum ECS potential of all three.** Compared to SS14, it is inferior in terms of evidence of compliance: in SS14, components, systems and cross-system events are confirmed by the guide and project code, and damage, hands/containment, tool use, atmosphere, power and round outcome intersect on the characters and objects of the station. RimWorld can match the breadth of game rules - jobs, skills, relationships, needs, supplies, health and incidents - but here the conclusion is still based on the external behavior of the game, and not an audit of its runtime. Relative to FTL, RimWorld appears to be broader in terms of entity families and social/work states; The FTL candidate, however, is built on a less variable ship layout. Qud rivals RimWorld in terms of breadth of actors and worlds to change, but its grid, mutation/faction AI, and procedural content make it difficult to estimate the share of logic that independent ECS queries will bring.

The conditional ranking should therefore remain separate: **based on confirmed ECS architecture and visibility of intersecting systems - SS14 ahead; in terms of raw mechanic breadth - Dwarf Fortress is ahead within this report, with Qud/RimWorld/SS14 forming the unresolved top group; FTL is easier to limit, but it is narrower in terms of changeable families of entities.** Cosmoteer can also get into the top group while maintaining a living constructor, modular damage, crew tasks and ship connectivity; Moreover, a significant part of its complexity is topology/pathfinding/physics, not ECS. This is a comparative judgment, not a measurement or a statement that commercial games are implemented on ECS.

**Recognizable cycle test:** RimWorld-slice remains exactly the story of the colony if the player distributes work and protection when there is a shortage of food, moves a physical resource, reacts to an incident and sees survivors/hunger in the outcome; without incident or choice, you get a generic job simulator. The proposed SS14 solo-engineer slice remains a station-emergency game, but loses its declared multiplayer/role-play basis, so it should be called that or bring back other players/significant role-playing participants. FTL maintains recognition through pause and crew orders, systems control and target/route selection. Qud requires mutation assembly and effects on NPCs/world material; one level of “kill enemies and take a relic” is not a Qud clone in itself.

The most underrated value of RimWorld is **global job assignment and AI**, where skills/priorities/schedules/availability/materials/reservations and paths intersect; the central scheduler can return the logic to the monolith. In SS14, abandoning multiplayer will require NPC job/role AI, and fidelity still carries atmospheric and power-network solvers. FTL has a simpler navigation graph, but requires crew assignment/routes and selection of enemy ship actions. Qud and DF are growing in goal-planning, faction/social reactions, navigation and content combinations. If these subsystems behave as the only global optimizers, ECS does not become a major value simply because of the number of entities.

Current preliminary decision on the hypothesis: **maximum breadth of mechanics in the colony family - Dwarf Fortress; best first recognizable clone within the family - RimWorld-like "first colony story"; SS14 shows the maximum proven ECS fit among the matched candidates; FTL is a more limited ship scenario, Qud is a serious, but not yet architecturally confirmed competitor; Leave ONI as an alternative for grid-heavy architecture**. There are no numerical estimates and perf results.
