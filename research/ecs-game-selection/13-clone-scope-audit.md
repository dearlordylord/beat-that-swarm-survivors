# Scope audit: recognizable clones among six ECS candidates

This audit tests six proposed bounded versions against the gameplay identity their creators describe: an SS14 solo emergency, a short FTL run, a small-grammar Baba puzzle, a solo Magicka challenge, a RimWorld mini-colony, and a Cosmoteer pre-battle builder. “Bounded” here means limiting maps, roles, encounters, rules, or campaign content; it does **not** assume a development-time or first-game schedule limit. It keeps the [evaluation contract](00-evaluation-contract.md)'s distinction between maximum ECS exposure and a recognizable first clone. The gameplay facts below come from publisher/developer pages and developer material. ECS comparisons and scope judgments are design inferences, not claims about proprietary game architecture.

## Outcome at a glance

| Proposed scope | Recognizable as… | Where the cut crosses into “inspired by” |
|---|---|---|
| **SS14 solo emergency** | A station-maintenance/emergency scenario using SS14-like mechanics | As a solo game it removes the round-based multiplayer role-play and player-to-player interaction that the official description and design pillars treat as central. Keep a crew with meaningful roles and interaction if the claim is an SS14 clone. |
| **FTL short run** | An FTL run segment, if it keeps beacon-to-beacon progression and ship/crew decisions between encounters | One standalone ship battle loses the run: exploration, event choices, accumulating damage/losses, and upgrades. A short route is still a bounded slice, not the whole campaign. |
| **Baba simple grammar** | A Baba-style puzzle level, if movable word blocks change object properties or the goal in a solvable room | Frozen rules plus block pushing is a Sokoban-like puzzle; the changing rule must affect the route or solution. One room is a core-mechanic slice, not a full campaign clone. |
| **Magicka solo challenge** | A slice of an actual Magicka mode | This is the cleanest exact mode match: the publisher describes a solo option and a wave-based challenge mode. Remove element-combination casting and it becomes a generic wizard arena; omit campaign/co-op and name it a challenge-mode slice, not the whole game. |
| **RimWorld mini-colony** | A bounded colony-story scenario, if individual colonists, work/resource choices, survival pressure, and a storyteller incident all matter | Without a consequential incident and pawn-specific behavior, it is a generic job/resource simulator. A fixed short scenario is not RimWorld’s full open-ended story generator or an original canonical win condition. |
| **Cosmoteer pre-battle builder** | The ship-design/creative part of Cosmoteer | As a complete game loop, a builder without a battle never tests whether design, crew, and damage matter. Add one tactical battle with working crew, targetable modules, and a result to make a recognizable Cosmoteer battle slice; that still is not its campaign. |

The clearest small *game-mode* slices are Magicka’s solo challenge and a single Baba puzzle whose rule manipulation changes play. FTL can remain recognizable as a shortened run if inter-encounter choices persist. The RimWorld mini-colony is conditional on preserving an authored story beat. The exact SS14-solo and Cosmoteer-builder-only versions should be described as inspired prototypes or subsystem slices. These classifications concern recognizability, not effort or value.

## What each original loop requires

### Space Station 14: a shift with other players

The official SS14 description frames the game as a round-based multiplayer role-playing game about an ordinary station shift going wrong. Players take crew or antagonist jobs with different duties and equipment; disasters, incompetence, and sabotage produce emergent choices. The official design pillars reinforce unpredictable escalating situations, a malleable station, interconnected simulation, and mechanics that naturally encourage interaction among players. ([SS14: About](https://spacestation14.com/about/about/); [SS14 Core Game Design](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/space-station-14/core-design.html))

For a bounded SS14 clone, keep a small station, a few differentiated crew roles, one shared incident, the job’s station work, and a round-level resolution. The incident may be fixed and the station may be one map; that bounds content without removing the social structure. A solo engineer repairing life support can retain tool use, power, atmosphere, damage, and repair mechanics, but the player cannot negotiate, cooperate, obstruct, or improvise with other players. That changes the original loop into a single-player station-emergency game. Simulated crew can restore some cross-role decisions, but replacing human social play with AI is still an adaptation, not evidence that solo play preserves SS14’s identity.

**ECS implication (inference):** SS14 is among the widest-overlap candidates here when crew, tools, containers, damage, atmosphere, power, and round state all participate in shared station events. Its strongest bounded cut must retain interaction among roles; shrinking it to one actor specifically removes part of the loop that makes those systems cohere. See the [SS14 candidate study](07-space-station.md) for the source audit of its actual ECS engine.

### FTL: a run, not one encounter

Subset’s official description emphasizes a ship and crew moving through a randomized galaxy, multiple solutions to encounters, crew orders, power distribution, weapon targeting, pausing to issue orders, text encounters, and permadeath. The developers’ launch-era interview describes the surrounding rhythm as travelling between star systems, encountering events, upgrading the ship, then handling combat inside the ship; it calls out how fire, doors, oxygen, and boarders combine into situations. ([Subset Games, FTL on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/); [Subset Games founders interviewed at launch](https://www.engadget.com/2012-09-19-the-joystiq-indie-pitch-ftl-faster-than-light.html))

Keep a compact route with more than one jump, at least one non-combat choice, ship and crew state that carry forward, a tactical encounter with pause/orders/power/targeting, and a terminal run outcome. Reuse a few encounter templates rather than copying the full event catalogue. A fixed ship layout is a content cut that preserves the combat loop. A single arena fight—even a good one—does not preserve the rogue-like run structure; call that a ship-combat prototype. A short route that ends after an authored climax is recognizable as a mini-run, not a full FTL campaign.

**ECS implication (inference):** FTL yields a dense, legible set of queries across crew, rooms, systems, hazards, and damage, with a modest topology. It offers less dynamic composition than SS14, RimWorld, or a fully modular Cosmoteer fight, but its bounded run naturally demonstrates persistent relations and state across phases. The [ship study](08-modular-ships.md) details the trade between this fixed layout and Cosmoteer's mutable one.

### Baba Is You: rules are movable objects

The developer-published game page says that rules appear as blocks in the level and that pushing them changes how the level works, including object properties and the goal. Creator Arvi Teikari’s GDC talk and slides describe the sentence form as object–verb–quality and explain that gameplay systems ask which rules exist and which objects they affect. ([Hempuli Oy, Baba Is You on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/736260/Baba_Is_You/); [GDC talk by creator Arvi Teikari](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026590/Reading-the-Rules-of-Baba); [creator’s slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/presentations/Reading%20the%20rules_Teikari_Arvi.pdf))

For one recognizable puzzle, keep ordinary movement/pushing, physical word pieces, a small sentence grammar, recomputation of active rules after a word moves, and one solution where that changed property or goal changes what the player can do. `BABA IS YOU`, `ROCK IS PUSH`, `WALL IS STOP`, and `FLAG IS WIN` are enough vocabulary for a designed room; one of those rules must be strategically changed rather than just parsed. Remove the campaign, conditional/advanced grammar, and broad level library. If the level is solved without pushing or changing a rule, it is only a puzzle with sentence tiles. A single room demonstrates the signature mechanic but is not a content-complete Baba clone.

**ECS implication (inference):** Baba is the sharpest test here of behavior changing when components are added or removed at runtime. It is narrow in entity families and relations, so it showcases dynamic composition more than maximum total ECS surface. The [rule-puzzle study](10-rule-puzzles.md) records the proposed one-room trace and its grid-resolution boundary.

### Magicka: the solo challenge is already an original mode

Paradox’s Magicka page describes an adventure campaign, a dynamic system for combining elements into spells, challenge mode with waves of enemies, and a single-player option for all modes. The publisher’s Steam page identifies the challenge as an unlockable wave-survival mode. ([Paradox, Magicka](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/magicka/about); [Arrowhead/Paradox, Magicka on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/42910/Magicka/))

That makes “solo challenge” a fair clone slice of a specific original mode, not a workaround invented for this project. Keep a controllable wizard, player-built combinations of elements, spell delivery into targets, waves that escalate or vary, and survival/score completion. The [original manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/42910/manuals/Magicka%20manual%20body%20--%20English.pdf) documents the element and delivery options. A small fixed arena and a small selection of elements are content cuts; fixed one-button spells are not, because combining elements is the advertised core. Co-op, campaign traversal, story and unlock progression can be omitted if the product is explicitly a solo challenge-mode slice. Do not claim this reproduces the full adventure campaign. Friendly fire can be omitted in a solo-only scenario unless allies are included; it is not necessary to make solo Magicka recognizable.

**ECS implication (inference):** Spell profiles, projectiles/areas, target status, and actor health create strong query overlap and lifecycle work, while remaining a relatively narrow arena simulation. It has less cross-domain breadth than SS14, RimWorld, or a builder-plus-battle Cosmoteer slice, but the proposed slice is already supported by an original mode. See the [systemic-sandbox study](04-systemic-sandbox.md) for the proposed cross-status gameplay trace.

### RimWorld: an authored episode inside a story generator

Ludeon describes RimWorld as a sci-fi colony story generator driven by an AI storyteller. Its official page starts with three shipwreck survivors and identifies colonist needs, moods, wounds, relationships, work capabilities, hostile events, crafting, and survival as part of the experience; the storyteller controls events such as raids and travelers. ([Ludeon, RimWorld on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/); [Ludeon on storyteller choices and events](https://ludeon.com/blog/2013/09/title-screen-update/))

Ludeon’s early developer walkthrough specifically combines pirate raids, time for the player to prepare, colonist work settings, construction/mining, and the option to capture and later recruit a raider. A separate development update describes physical stockpiles with priorities, hauling between them, and cooking/food supply as distinct work. These primary examples support a compact causal story: shortage prompts work assignment, material moves, a threat arrives, and the player chooses how to protect particular people and stores. ([Ludeon, First Look Video](https://ludeon.com/blog/2013/09/first-look-video-released/); [Ludeon, Alpha 1 stockpiling and hauling](https://ludeon.com/blog/2014/01/alpha-1-released/))

A bounded mini-colony should keep several distinct colonists, assignable work, a physical resource bottleneck, a basic need or mood state, and one storyteller-like incident whose timing or response changes the colony outcome. One small map, a fixed starting group, one crop/meal chain, and one raid or visitor are reasonable content cuts. A fixed scenario can stand in for the storyteller, but the incident needs to create a decision and story consequence; a scripted combat wave unrelated to work and scarcity will read as a tower-defense game. RimWorld does not have to be forced into a canonical short victory condition: show a bounded episode outcome (who ate, who was hurt, what was built, who joined or left) and label it a scenario, not the game’s universal win condition. Remove all pawn-specific work/needs, storyteller incidents, or consequence-bearing character relations and the recognizable colony story is gone.

**ECS implication (inference):** This is among the broadest single-player compositions in the shortlist: colonists, jobs, items, buildings, needs, relationships, and events can share entities across systems. Work allocation, reachability, and pathfinding are substantial specialized problems, but without a schedule constraint they are costs to account for, not grounds for automatically demoting the candidate. See the [colony study](03-colony.md) for its proposed episode and solver trade-offs.

### Cosmoteer: design must face a battle

Walternate Realities describes Cosmoteer as both a ship builder and a real-time tactical combat game. The official page says that players shape ships by placing modules, crew operate systems and carry munitions, modules can be individually targeted and destroyed, and ships can split apart. It also explicitly offers a Creative Mode where players build freely; Career adds exploration, contracts, loot, upgrades and combat. The developer’s construction update explains how crew-carried resources make building and repair part of the simulation and why unrestricted instantaneous repairs during battle would undermine combat decisions. ([Walternate Realities, Cosmoteer on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/799600/Cosmoteer/); [developer update on crew construction and repair](https://store.steampowered.com/news/posts/?appids=799600&enddate=1688070032&feed=steam_community_announcements))

If the bounded target is explicitly Creative Mode, a builder-only slice can be an honest clone of that submode: place and inspect a useful small ship. It is not a clone of Cosmoteer’s full play loop. For a gameplay clone, keep the builder decision and then put that ship into a real-time battle where crew workload, power, module targeting/damage, and victory or defeat reflect the design. One authored opponent, a small grid, one weapon and one damaged system bound content while preserving the design→battle feedback loop. Remove the battle and its consequences and the output is a ship editor; remove the builder and crew behavior and it is generic ship combat.

**ECS implication (inference):** A build-plus-battle slice has very high compositional opportunity across crew, jobs, carried resources, modules, power, damage, and hazards. Grid topology, connectivity, movement/pathfinding, and physics remain specialized host work. That burden does not invalidate it under the stated priority; it makes the boundary between ECS and spatial solvers a central design choice. See the [ship study](08-modular-ships.md) for the proposed battle boundary.

## Comparison under maximum ECS priority

Do not select a candidate only because it is quickest to make playable. The user's stated objective is maximum ECS exposure, so development duration is not a ranking criterion here. Keep two separate answers:

- **Broadest ECS workload among bounded clones (inference):** SS14 with multiple meaningful roles in one chaotic shift; Cosmoteer with a real builder-to-battle loop; and a RimWorld mini-colony with work, needs, scarce resources, relationships, and an incident form the high-breadth group. SS14 has the strongest direct evidence of an ECS engine from the separate [SS14 candidate study](07-space-station.md), but its solo variant discards core social play. Cosmoteer’s crew/module composition is dense, while its grid and physics solvers are outside the ECS. RimWorld offers broad character/work/event composition without multiplayer, but its scheduler and movement are major non-ECS subsystems.
- **Strongest narrow, identifiable ECS demonstrations:** Baba shows mutable behavior membership; Magicka shows composable effects and overlapping status queries; FTL shows stateful crew/system/hazard interaction across a compact run. Those are compelling slices, but their narrower domain coverage should not be mistaken for the maximum ECS workload.

For the proposals exactly as phrased, Magicka solo challenge is a mode-level clone, Baba simple grammar is a mechanic-level puzzle slice, and FTL short run is a shortened campaign segment if it retains route/event/state continuity. RimWorld is a recognizable scenario only with a living colonist-and-incident story. SS14 solo emergency and Cosmoteer pre-battle-only should not be presented as full clones. If maximum ECS takes precedence, expand those two just enough to preserve their defining loop—role interaction for SS14 and a consequential battle for Cosmoteer—instead of cutting the identity away to optimize a smaller prototype.

None of these mechanics descriptions proves an original game’s implementation uses ECS. The explicit SS14 ECS evidence is about its public engine/game source; the remaining ECS rankings here are architectural recommendations inferred from the game rules. This audit read the candidate reports and primary gameplay sources; it did not run any game, implement a slice, or benchmark performance.
