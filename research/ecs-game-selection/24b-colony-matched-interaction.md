# 24b — matched RimWorld colony interaction: rebuild the storm door

**Purpose.** This is a matched scenario for the station repair trace: a pawn and a scarce item must reach a damaged barrier, restore a safe route, change a hazard/need outcome, and let independent observers report the committed result. It answers the solver-asymmetry challenge in [20](20-synthesis-skeptical-review.md). The ECS layout below is a proposed Bendvy design, not a claim about RimWorld internals. No code, game, or benchmark was run.

## What the sources establish

Ludeon describes RimWorld resources as items physically present on the map, stored through designated zones with item filters and priorities; haulers fill higher-priority stockpiles, and the game distinguishes work types such as cooking from general hauling ([Ludeon, Alpha 1 stockpiling](https://ludeon.com/blog/2014/01/alpha-1-released/)). A Ludeon developer also clarified that prioritizing work on a blueprint can queue a material-hauling task, rather than executing the whole construction job end to end ([developer reply on queued jobs](https://ludeon.com/forums/index.php?topic=50833.0)). These sources support a visible job/haul/construction chain and real material access choices.

Ludeon’s game description presents colony stories through psychology, climate, medicine, interpersonal relationships, trade, and combat ([RimWorld on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/)). Its 1.6 announcement says late-game pathfinding was batched and multithreaded as a production optimization ([Ludeon, Update 1.6](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/)). That is evidence of pathfinding as a real domain cost in the production game. It does not show that a small fixed map requires that optimization, that work allocation is one monolithic planner, or how the original runtime divides needs, hauling, jobs, and construction.

Door repair jobs also exist: a Ludeon forum report and developer reply discuss a door-repair path in the game ([repair-job discussion](https://ludeon.com/forums/index.php?topic=15288.0)). Those materials do **not** establish that native repair consumes scarce construction material. I therefore do not charge steel for a vanilla repair. The matched scarcity scenario below is explicitly a **replacement-build adaptation**: construct a new door at the damaged doorway, using scarce building material. Its player-facing outcome resembles repairing the barrier, but the job verb and material cost differ from a literal repair action.

## Proposed matched scenario: Cold Pantry

Use a small colony map with a warm food room, an exposed vestibule, and an exterior doorway whose door is damaged and stuck open. The open doorway lets the cold hazard reach the food room and makes the shortest route to the meal stockpile unsafe. One hungry pawn has the relevant construction skill; the prioritized stockpile holds only one matching material lot, enough for one replacement blueprint in this scenario. The player designates a replacement door at the damaged opening and raises that job above routine hauling. The proposed one-day objective is to restore a safe, traversable route to the meal stockpile before the pawn's hunger or cold exposure becomes critical.

All numbers, temperatures, thresholds, room links, and success conditions in this scenario are invented for comparison. Source-backed facts are the physical stockpile/hauling choice and the broader need, climate, and colony-story mechanics. The replacement blueprint, its assigned material, and the exact thermal consequence are this proposed game model. The scenario does not assert that RimWorld's repair job consumes building material.

### Player trace

1. The player designates the replacement door and prioritizes its haul/build chain. The only matching material lot is visible in the stockpile; the hungry pawn is also eligible for a meal action. In this proposed scheduler, eating and hauling compete for the pawn reservation: if the meal action wins first, delivery waits; this trace gives the urgent build chain the pawn, so eating waits. This is a deliberate scenario rule, not a claim about RimWorld's exact job-arbitration internals.
2. The blueprint's material requirement exposes a separate haul-to-blueprint task. The allocator selects an eligible hauler and reserves the worker, source stack, material amount, and blueprint destination. A second job cannot silently claim the same material lot.
3. The pawn travels to the stockpile, picks up the reserved material, and carries it to the blueprint. The delivery task completes before the construction task is assigned; hunger and cold continue to advance during both trips.
4. A second work-selection pass assigns the construction task and reserves the worker and target. The same pawn can continue from the blueprint if still eligible, or the allocator can choose another builder. Construction progress then advances, with need updates continuing independently.
5. When the replacement door completes, the doorway is again traversable on demand and can close against the cold. The small room/climate solver updates its cold exchange; the pathfinder recomputes access through the new door. The pawn then reaches the food stockpile and eats. The goal is achieved only after the passage is usable and hunger/cold state is safe under the proposed threshold.

This preserves the colony loop more faithfully than a one-shot repair button: player priority, worker selection, physical material movement, construction progress, hazard, needs, and access all affect the outcome. The technical model may still choose a small, explicit staged action; the original game's source materials do not warrant attributing its internal boundaries to RimWorld.

## Proposed entity families and exact component access

These are component names for the Bendvy consumer, not names found in RimWorld source. `WorkLedger` is a world resource holding reservation records and the action stage; designated orders, physical stacks, and the doorway remain entities so the design does not erase map objects into an inventory number. When the ledger needs to refer to a selected entity, the proposal stores its typed `Handle<Schema>` value. Bendvy defines that handle as `Data`, with a world namespace and full generational key. This does not imply that every ordinary query callback currently has a convenient binding for the current row's handle; that is an API ergonomics constraint, separate from whether a handle is a `Data` value.

| Family | Proposed components / fields | Read by | Written by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pawn** | `GridPosition`, `Health`, `Needs{Hunger,ColdExposure}`, `WorkCapability{Construction,Hauling}`, `WorkPriority`, `CarryState`, `JobState`, `Skill{Construction}` | Need update, work eligibility and scoring, path request, hauling, construction progress, goal check | Need update writes `Needs` and, if needed, `Health`; assignment writes `JobState`; movement writes `GridPosition`; pickup/drop changes `CarryState`; work progress writes `JobState`/`WorkProgress` |
| **Material stack** | `GridPosition`, `MaterialKind`, `StackCount`, `Forbidden/Allowed` | Stockpile/material selection, reservation check, hauling/pickup, job validation | Pickup moves or splits the stack; final debit writes `StackCount` or stages despawn; handoff changes `CarriedBy/StoredIn` relation at a barrier. Reservation ownership lives in the work ledger/relation, not a second copy on the stack row. |
| **Construction order / blueprint** | `GridPosition`, `BuildKind{ReplacementDoor}`, `RequiredMaterial`, `WorkRemaining`, `Priority`, `OrderState`, derived `DeliveryTask` and `BuildTask` states | Haul-task selection, construction-task selection, reservation, path target selection, progress/finalization | Delivery and construction phases update their respective task states; work progress updates `WorkRemaining`; completion retires the order or marks it complete |
| **Door / barrier** | `GridPosition`, `Condition`, `Open`, `PassableWhenOpen`, `ThermalBoundary` | Navigation topology, environment boundary, damage/status, goal readers | Construction finalization changes `Condition` and available door behavior; it remains the current row of a typed target query |
| **Room / climate field** | `RoomId`, `Temperature`, `Warmth/ColdBoundary` or a small grid/zone field | Exposure and climate step; UI/goal observation | The specialized climate step updates temperature or zone exchange after the door state commits |
| **Meal stack** | `GridPosition`, `FoodKind`, `Nutrition`, `StackCount`, optional `StoredIn` | Meal selection, stockpile and path requests, eating/goal checks | Eating decrements quantity; a pawn need query lowers hunger after successful consumption |
| **Threat / weather** | Resource or bounded field: `StormActive`, `OutsideTemperature`, phase/time | Climate, path cost, goal/alert systems | Scenario clock/incident system advances it; this is a small proposed solver input, not a claim about RimWorld's grid representation |
| **Work ledger resource** | In-flight worker/order/target/material `Handle<Schema>` values; reservation owner and quantity; pending outcome; material debit/refund status | Dispatch, reservation, typed row passes, inspection | Recognized current-row + whole-resource transactions update it; it records staged decisions while the committed relation graph remains the source of current ownership/state |

The useful overlaps are specific. `Pawn.GridPosition` is read by movement, path selection, hazard exposure, and meal reachability. `Pawn.Needs` is updated by the need system and used by job scoring and goal checks. `MaterialStack.GridPosition/MaterialKind/StackCount` is read by hauling and construction selection. `Door.GridPosition/Condition/Open` is read by movement topology and climate exchange and written by the construction result. These are candidate shared queries; the table does not claim the commercial game uses those components or query boundaries.

### Relations and lifecycle

Use `AssignedTo(Pawn, ConstructionOrder)`, `Targets(ConstructionOrder, Door)`, `Reserves(ConstructionOrder, MaterialStack)`, `StoredIn(MaterialStack, Stockpile)`, and `Carries(Pawn, MaterialStack)`. They represent job ownership, target identity, resource contention, stockpile location, and the haul handoff. The relation graph is the canonical committed ownership/target view; the ledger holds handles only for an in-flight decision until its relation changes commit at a barrier. On cancel, target loss, pawn incapacitation, or completed construction, remove or deactivate the applicable relations and clear the pending reservation owner. Do not mirror every committed edge as another component field without a cleanup rule.

The job allocator and pathfinder are not ECS systems by definition. They are specialized algorithms called by a system or host adapter; their results feed typed queries. Likewise, temperature spreading across a grid/zone is one small domain solver. Count ECS value in the independent component queries that integrate these results and update shared entities, not in every branch or helper inside those solvers.

## Independent queries and specialized calls

The following query registrations are a proposed implementation map. A “query” here means a distinct family-oriented data pass; it does not assert an original RimWorld query implementation.

| Proposed pass/query | Component selection and reads | Writes / independent purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Pawn needs/exposure** | Current `Pawn` row: read `GridPosition`, `Needs`, `Health`; read current room/climate observation | Write current pawn's `Needs`/`Health`. This runs independently of the construction job and records the storm's cost even while the pawn works. |
| **Eligible worker observation** | Pawn rows: read `WorkCapability`, `WorkPriority`, `Skill`, `Needs`, `JobState`, `GridPosition` | Supplies candidates to the allocator. The selection adapter must retain/pass each candidate's `Handle<Schema>`; this is a handle-binding question for the current-row API. Read-only; it does not reserve a job or path. |
| **Job/material observation** | `ConstructionOrder` rows: read `Priority`, `BuildKind`, `RequiredMaterial`, `OrderState`; `MaterialStack` rows: read `MaterialKind`, `StackCount`, stockpile permission/position | Supplies open work and eligible stock to matching. The adapter carries each row's `Handle<Schema>` alongside its observed values. These are separate family observations, not one implicit relation join. |
| **Relation-reference observation** | Read `AssignedTo`, `Targets`, `Reserves`, `StoredIn`, and `Carries`; relation projection returns detached typed handles | Supplies linked entity identities to the allocator, path requests, or UI. It is a real read-only relation query, but it does not join component rows into a multi-row writer and is not counted as another independent component-family pass. |
| **Assignment commit** | Current eligible `Pawn` row: read/write `JobState`; read/write whole `WorkLedger` resource; selected worker/order/material handles enter through the matching adapter | Commit one pawn's assignment and reservation record keyed by typed entity handles in a recognized `pair(write, resource_write)` transaction. It does not also write the order, stack, and door rows. The recognized callback's current-row handle binding needs an explicit supported route or API addition; `Handle<Schema>` being `Data` does not itself supply that binding. |
| **Material reservation/debit** | Current `MaterialStack` row: read `MaterialKind`, `StackCount`; at debit, write `StackCount`; read/write whole `WorkLedger`; selected stack/order handles enter through the matching adapter | Reservation first records the chosen stack handle in the ledger (and exposes a `Reserves` relation at its structural barrier); a later typed stack pass debits the selected current stack and updates ledger stage. Current-row component + whole-resource pairing is supported; selecting an arbitrary target by relation inside this callback is not. A typed handle can be passed as data where the callback has an explicit input route, but current-row identity binding remains a separate question. |
| **Travel and carry** | Current `Pawn` row: read `JobState`, `CarryState`, `GridPosition`; route is supplied by pathfinder | Write current pawn position/carry stage. `Carries`/`StoredIn` relation edits become visible at an explicit structural barrier. This does not directly mutate the other stack row. |
| **Construction progress** | Current active `Pawn` or order row: read skill, job stage, work remaining; target handle comes from the prepared relation observation/ledger stage | Write current row's progress. Do not claim that the same callback can follow the relation and mutate the door and material entities. A small consumer can keep progress on the order entity and finalize the target in a later typed pass. |
| **Door finalization** | Current `Door` row: read/write `Condition`, `Open`, `ThermalBoundary`; read/write whole `WorkLedger` | Set the restored door state and record the target-applied stage in one current-door + whole-resource transaction. A query over a different row cannot join from the assigned pawn's relation and write this door directly. |
| **Climate / topology observation** | Door rows read into a bounded room graph; room/grid fields read by the climate step | The climate solver updates room/zone temperature; a navigation graph/pathfinder observes the committed door mode and recalculates reachability. These are distinct domain steps, not additional independent component-query credit. |
| **Post-commit readers** | Buffered `PassageRestored` / `ConstructionFailed` events; independent reader cursors | Climate/UI/log/goal readers react after the publishing transaction commits. A buffered event informs; it does not synchronously approve the construction. |

The job allocator can remain one bounded matching routine, or be split between candidate selection and reservation if the game needs that distinction. Neither option proves a monolithic original scheduler. The proposed trace has a haul task and a separate construction task, following Ludeon's source clarification. Keep the evidence fair: job priority, skill eligibility, reservation conflict, material-source choice, construction progress, and pathfinding are required work in this proposed scenario, but only the row passes above are counted as ECS query surfaces.

### Every specialized call in this scenario

Treat these as explicit solver boundaries in the design, not recovered RimWorld internals:

1. **Haul-task selection:** when the material-required blueprint is issued, select an eligible hauling worker and expose the haul-to-blueprint task. Ludeon's developer notes explicitly distinguish material delivery from the construction work itself.
2. **Haul reservation check:** claim the worker, source stack/amount, and blueprint delivery slot. If any is already reserved, decline this assignment or select another candidate.
3. **Material-source selection:** find an allowed stack of the required kind, honor stockpile rules, and select a source/amount. Ludeon's source supports physical map items and prioritized stockpiles; the exact chooser is unspecified.
4. **Pathfinding call A:** route the assigned hauler from its current tile to the selected material stack, respecting doors, obstacles, allowed area, and the active cold hazard cost.
5. **Pathfinding call B:** after pickup, route the loaded pawn from the stack to the construction order. If the door/obstacle changes or the route becomes invalid, request a fresh route rather than assuming the old path remains valid.
6. **Build-task selection:** after material delivery, choose an eligible builder for the separate construction task. It may be the same pawn; if it is another pawn, make the route to the supplied blueprint an explicit path request rather than assuming it is free.
7. **Build reservation and job step:** reserve the target/order and worker, validate skill and staged input, and advance construction progress. When work completes, prepare a door-finalization attempt.
8. **Climate update:** run the deliberately small room/zone heat-exchange step after the barrier state changes. A production-scale grid/climate solver is outside the slice and must not be credited as an independent ECS query.
9. **Meal action and food reservation:** once construction releases the pawn, select the meal action and reserve an available meal stack. If the pawn is still reserved for work or no food is available, defer this action; the ECS need update continues regardless.
10. **Pathfinding call C:** after the door commits and topology is refreshed, route the pawn through the restored doorway to its selected meal stack. This call is a visible consequence of the interaction, not part of the material planner.

Candidate generation, reservation arbitration, source selection, and routes can be optimized or fused later. The trace counts them as separate *logical decisions* so their cost is visible; it does not claim the original game invokes exactly ten named methods or that each is globally centralized.

## Refusal, commit boundaries, and current Bendvy

**Refusal before writes.** If no allowed material unit exists, the target order is already reserved, the pawn is unavailable, or no path reaches the stack/site, the command is refused before assigning the job or consuming material. The UI explains “no available material,” “target reserved,” or “no route.” This is a normal checked action rejection, not transaction rollback. If two pawns compete for the final unit, the reservation ledger gives the first successful reservation ownership; the other gets no duplicate assignment.

**Target disappears after work starts.** Recheck the order and door before finalization. If the door/blueprint is missing before commit, mark the job cancelled, release reservations, and do not publish `PassageRestored`. If material has already been debited by a prior successful query, a later failure does not magically undo it; the ledger must enter `RefundDue` and a compensating stack query must restore it, or the game must explicitly accept the cost as spent. Keep the failure visible as a cancelled/failed build, not a success event.

**Late failure is not a whole-tick rollback.** A same-system query transaction can roll back its own journaled row/resource writes on a callback failure. Once a prior system has committed a material debit or work progress, a later target query or structural barrier failure does not reverse it. Relation edits and component insert/remove commands also become visible only at a barrier; a late barrier rejection cannot be treated as if earlier successful systems never ran. Therefore:

- reserve first, debit at a documented phase, and keep a durable ledger state for pending/cancelled/refund work;
- publish `PassageRestored` only after the target state and resource policy have both succeeded;
- let later readers display a committed `ConstructionFailed` or `PassageRestored` fact; do not send success UI/audio from a callback that can still fail;
- if the product requires all-or-nothing across Pawn + MaterialStack + Door and structural relation changes, use a separately validated composite adapter or redesign the action into accepted visible phases. Current ordinary App does not provide that multi-entity transaction.

Bendvy's `Handle<Schema>` is `Data` and contains a namespace plus full generational key ([handle declaration](../../../bendvy/src/ecs/world.bend#L10-L15)). The supported recognized App query can pair a writer for the **current row** with a whole-resource read/write in one transaction, and `pair` can combine declared capabilities. That is useful for `Pawn.JobState + WorkLedger`, `MaterialStack.StackCount + WorkLedger`, or `Door.State + WorkLedger` as separate typed passes. It does not combine those three different entity rows into one atomic writer. The recognized body receives declared capabilities and explicit arguments; its current-row handle is not an ordinary granted component value. Relation views can return detached handles and snapshots read-only; hierarchy traversal is a specialized tree route, not a generic `AssignedTo → Targets` join. See the corrected [cross-entity audit](12-cross-entity-access-audit.md), [recognized declaration](../../../bendvy/src/ecs/recognized-query-declaration.bend#L49-L78), [recognized App registration](../../../bendvy/src/ecs/recognized-app-component.bend#L87-L102), [relation query contract](../../../bendvy/src/ecs/relation-query.bend#L23-L38), and [deferred relation barrier](../../../bendvy/src/ecs/ordinary-app-relation.bend#L140-L173).

If `AssignedTo`, `Targets`, `Reserves`, or `Carries` is changed as a deferred relation operation, place an explicit barrier before a phase that reads the new edge. A typed entity handle is itself a `Data` value and may be passed through an explicit supported argument/carrier route; that fact does not create a current-row handle binding or arbitrary selector/join capability in the ordinary App callback. The `WorkLedger` is useful for allocation/reservation decisions; it must not be used to imply the entities were changed atomically.

The event readers in this trace are runtime state. Do not infer checkpoint persistence for their reader cursors or the deferred command/event queues: those runtime queues/cursors need their own save-and-restore policy or must be recreated from saved world state.

## What the matched trace says about solver fairness

The colony scenario has more work orchestration than the station's direct repair attempt: worker eligibility, job selection, conflict reservation, stockpile/source choice, two material-haul routes, construction work, and then a route to the goal. This is a real burden. It does not erase the independent `Needs`, `Stack`, `Pawn`, `Door`, `Climate`, and post-commit observer queries proposed above.

The station scenario carries different specialized calls: tool/user/target validation, power/network propagation, atmospheric or room-pressure propagation, spatial collision/navigation, and a timed round/evacuation update. Its atmosphere/power algorithms are integrated with ECS entities but are not thereby ordinary independent queries. Apply the same accounting to both: count each domain solver as an integration cost and count only actual independent component-family passes as ECS query surface. Source 1.6 supports treating pathfinding as relevant RimWorld work, while not supporting an assumption that the fixed scenario needs production-scale multithreaded navigation. Without a prototype or original source audit, this matched trace narrows the comparison; it does not decide SS14 vs. RimWorld by arithmetic.
