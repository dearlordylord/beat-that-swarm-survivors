# Hypothesis 8: FTL vs Cosmoteer for the Fullest ECS Demonstration

**Hypothesis 8 of 10.** The comparison tests two different results: maximum ECS coverage and the most realistic first completed clone on the current Bendvy. The game mechanics from primary materials are described below; ECS models and proposed slices - design conclusions. The sources do not prove that FTL or Cosmoteer use ECS.

## Conclusion

**Among the two ship candidates, Cosmoteer gives more variable ECS composition.** The player builds the ship from modules and passages; an autonomous crew maintains weapons and carries ammunition; individual parts can be targeted and destroyed; fire and module failure spread the consequences throughout the ship. One combat chain traverses the components of Crew, Objectives, Location, Health, Modules, Power, Ammo, Hazards, and Ship. The current Cosmoteer Steam page also explicitly states that entire ships can fall apart. This is a conclusion from the mechanics, not an assessment of the source code.

**For the first bounded slice on Bendvy, an FTL-like tactical mini-campaign is safer.** Its rooms can be set up in small fixed patterns, and control can be reduced to pausing, energy distribution, crew orders and target selection. Such a game still uses multiple overlapping ECS systems, but does not require first building a freeform geometry editor, ship physics, a dense space simulator, and a crew spatial navigation editor.

Compared to these two games, the maximum dynamic composition remains with Cosmoteer, while FTL is better limited to a fixed scheme. This does not yet make FTL the leader in the fullest ECS demonstration among all ten hypotheses: SS14 has an ECS approach confirmed by the authors, and Baba Is You gives direct user choice that changes the properties of entities. Therefore, the strongest FTL version below frames it as a complete ship example with several overlapping queries, rather than as the clear winner of the entire study.

## What do sources confirm?

Subset Games describes FTL as having a crew and ship travel through a randomly generated galaxy, managing the power of systems, giving orders to the crew, selecting targets for weapons, and the ability to freeze time for tactical orders. The page also lists procedural encounters, hundreds of text events, ship upgrades, and permadeath. This shows the decision loop and the scope of the campaign, but does not reveal the structure of the engine. ([FTL on Steam, page of developer and publisher Subset Games](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/); [official page of FTL](https://www.subsetgames.com/ftl.html))

Walternate Realities describes Cosmoteer as a game with editable ship shape and layout, modules, individually simulated crew, assignable roles and priorities. The current Steam description states that a single module can be targeted and destroyed, and ships can split into multiple pieces; Physical combat, procedural galaxy, contracts, co-op, PvP and workshop are also announced there. ([Cosmoteer on Steam, page of developer and publisher Walternate Realities](https://store.steampowered.com/app/799600/Cosmoteer/))

Developer diaries explain how these mechanics intersect: an early prototype had crew automatically find working systems and manually carry ammunition between storage and a gun; passageways allowed faster movement. ([Walternate Realities, Crew & Ammo, 2011](https://blog.cosmoteer.net/2011/02/starship-builder-crew-ammo.html)) A later devlog describes one-way conveyor belts, different speeds when moving with/against their direction, two-tile-wide corridors and crew choosing a fast route. ([Walternate Realities, Cosmoteer 0.12.0, 2017](https://blog.cosmoteer.net/2017/06/cosmoteer-0120-missiles-fires-and-more.html)) Keep the modern game separate from historical evidence: a **2012** StarWright prototype post says destroying parts could split a ship; this supports an early mechanic, not the current Cosmoteer version. The current Cosmoteer Steam page independently confirms ships breaking into parts. ([Walternate Realities, StarWright — Ship Combat, historical note](https://blog.cosmoteer.net/2012/02/starwright-ship-combat.html); [current Cosmoteer page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/799600/Cosmoteer/)) Fires in Cosmoteer spread through doors and open interior spaces, slow crew down and can kill them; crew independently collect a fire extinguisher, carry it over and extinguish the fire. ([Walternate Realities, Cosmoteer 0.12.0](https://blog.cosmoteer.net/2017/06/cosmoteer-0120-missiles-fires-and-more.html))

These are facts about the game rules and product description. Neither the Steam page nor these devlogs give any reason to claim that the internals of a commercial game are built on ECS, archetypes or data-oriented storage. The word "individually simulated" describes the crew as a game object, not an engine architecture.

## ECS load: what is naturally composed

| | FTL-like circuit | Cosmoteer-like scheme |
|---|---|---|
| Entities | ships, rooms and systems, crew members, projectiles, fire/breach, items | ships, hull cells/modules, crew, ammunition, shells, damage, fire/heat, tasks and resources |
| Overlapping queries | the crew participates in movement, work at the station, repair, extinguishing, treatment and boarding combat; ship systems cross power, weapon commands and damage | one crew member is involved in movement, mission selection, supply, system maintenance, repair/extinguishing and taking damage; module - in power, guidance, damage and victory condition |
| Lineup changes | dead/hired crew, added system/upgrade, launched threat | module construction changes, damage/destruction, new projectile or item, new crew mission, fire/heat and possible ship separation |
| Relationships | the ship owns the room and crew; crew assigned to system/task; weapon aims at enemy system | part belongs to the ship; the crew is in the room and assigned to the task; ammunition is transferred to the module; module/ship are associated by neighborhood or ownership; the projectile is aimed at a part |
| Phases and events | order → shot/reload → hit → damage → repair/fire/suffocation → outcome of the meeting | target selection → movement/guidance → collision → geometry/module damage → danger propagation → crew planning → movement/repair → connectivity recalculation → battle outcome |
| Basic non-ECS load | Warship UI, event and content tables, simple route map | geometry editing, layout validation, grid/graph of passages and doors, pathfinding, spatial combat/collisions, geometric rendering and, if complete, network |

The ECS utility of Cosmoteer is broader because multiple independently changing entities share common characteristics across different responsibilities. The difference between a repair crew and a cartridge delivery crew can be expressed by the `Crew + Position + Health + Order + Carrying` kit and system availability rather than two monolithic classes. Movement, job selection, supply, repair and damage systems cross the same train with different requests. The module is also not limited to “weapons”: `Module + PowerConsumer + Health + Targetable` can share the mechanics of power, damage and targeting with other subsystems.

But not all topology should become ECS entities. Pixels, cells, doors, reachability and neighboring rooms form a grid/graph that requires specialized neighbor access, flood fill, connectivity components or pathfinder. An ECS relationship like "crew assigned to module" is useful for referencing and clearing a target, but does not replace passage pathfinding or calculate cutting the ship into coherent parts. If every cell in a game is an entity, and frequent cell traversal controls the entire combat, the demonstration effectively becomes a grid solver's task.

FTL gives fewer combinations, but they stand out well. The rooms and layout of the ship are pre-set; local systems deal with crew, damage, oxygen/hazard and power levels. There is already a useful composition in that the same crew participates in several systems, and one attack affects the body, the system and the actors in the room. Roster growth over the course of a campaign is primarily driven by new crew members, upgrades, and event content rather than random ship rebuilds.

## A specific chain of causes and reactions

An example for a Cosmoteer slice, intentionally small and deterministic:

1. An enemy projectile passes through a section of armor and enters the reactor room. The projectile is a separate entity with `Position`, `Velocity`, `Damage`, `Lifetime`, `Team` and a reference to the target/source. Interception and collision use a spatial model that must be written separately.
2. The hit system reduces the health of the cell and reactor. The power system sees that the reactor is no longer producing power: the shield receives less power and shuts down, and the player's gun loses the ability to charge. Mechanics test common entities, but use different sets of components.
3. Reactor destruction adds fire/heat and publishes `ModuleDamaged`/`FireStarted`. Individual readers can update the combat log, defeat/crash condition and host indicator. The external UI only receives the confirmed observation after the transaction is completed.
4. The work manager assigns the nearest available engineer to put out the fire or repair the reactor. This is a change to `Order/AssignedTarget`; The navigation system searches for a path in the aisle graph, after which the movement system guides the crew. Path, congestion, doors and obstacles - a graph/spatial problem.
5. Doors limit the spread of fire and change the route. The fire spreads only along the permissible topology; Crew, health, speed, fire extinguisher availability, and crew mission overlap across multiple systems. If the crew is successful, the repair restores the power component after the barrier; if not, the shield and weapon remain disabled, the enemy destroys the player’s body - defeat.
6. At the same time, the loss of a connecting part can cut the housing into disconnected components. The connectivity recalculation decides whether the segments remain one ship or become separate objects. This is a strong ECS ​​story for the entity lifecycle, but the connectivity component itself is not a normal ECS query and will require a separate graph step.

This is not a reconstruction of the internal code of the original. This is a design scenario derived from published rules about modular damage, fires, and crew performance. To complete the game, the user must choose which part of the enemy ship to aim the weapon at and what task to give the crew in the accident; the action should change the state of the modules, and the result should be visible in battle.

## Two closed first cuts

**Cosmoteer-like slice with maximum ECS coverage:** On-screen ship designer on a fixed grid, one ready ship against one enemy, one laser/cannon, reactor and shield, three crew members, passages, hull damage and fire. The player places several modules and passages, starts a battle in real time with a pause, selects an enemy module as a target and assigns the crew to repair or extinguish. Victory - the reactor or all combat capabilities of the enemy are destroyed; defeat - its own reactor is destroyed or the entire crew is incapacitated. These are the terms of the projected clone, not statements about the exact terms of Cosmoteer. Keep ship and bullet kinematics simple, use one fixed enemy blueprint, limit the layout to about 8x8 tiles. The editor must support placement, rotation, occupancy, connecting passages, and composition inspection. Even with these limitations, the cost of UI and geometry checks is noticeable.

**The strongest FTL-like slice for ECS is a short run, not a single fight.** One fight with crew states, systems power, hazards, and targeting may be a complete tactical prototype, but I wouldn't call it an FTL clone: ​​it lacks transitions between waypoints, saving crew/damage between fights, and choosing how to spend the reward before the next fight. Completed mini-run: three steps through a compact beacon graph, which is built from a seed and several node templates; before a fight, choosing a safe or risky path; two template battles with different goals; after the first battle - the choice to spend scrap on hull repairs or additional power; the final battle and the outcome of the race. A couple of enemy templates are enough for a repeatable run without hundreds of hand-written story encounters. In combat there are fixed rooms, weapons, shield, engine, reactor, oxygen, two crew members, pause, energy distribution, target selection according to the enemy system, door operation and crew movement. Victory - a living ship reaches the exit after the final battle; defeat - the hull is destroyed or the entire crew is killed. This is an original mini-run in the form of FTL, not a full campaign or retread of the original story. ([Subset Games, FTL](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/))

For this slice to be a demonstration of ECS, and not separate arrays of crew/systems/doors, we need at least the following **general data in intersecting queries**:

| Common components/links | Who carries them | Which independent systems intersect? |
|---|---|---|
| `Team`, `Targetable`, `Damageable` | crew, room/system targets and unibody; the projectile carries `Team` and `Target` | choice of enemy target, hit/damage, repair or treatment, condition of ship death and the result of the battle |
| `RoomLocation` as connection to the room + `Health` | crew, installed system, active fire/breach; the door links to a couple of rooms | crew movement and mission selection; applying damage/repair; fire/low oxygen exposure testing; linking an aimed strike to the system |
| `DoorEndpoints` + replacement component `Open`/`Sealed` | the essence of the door connects two rooms | pathfinder uses an edge to route the crew, and a fire/air system to allow or block propagation; the player's click changes the result of both queries |
| `Order`/`AssignedTarget` + `Carrying` | crew; target - damaged system, fire extinguisher or station | job-selection creates an assignment, navigation moves the same crew, service/repair completes the task and updates the same module/hazard |

For example, one crew with `Team + RoomLocation + Health + Order` enters a movement request, station work request, repair and fire damage check; it does not need to be copied into separate role arrays. The door with `DoorEndpoints + Open` participates in navigation and hazard transfer requests, and its state changes both results. One module with `Team + RoomLocation + Damageable + Targetable + PowerDemand` intersects with power distribution, enemy target selection, damage and crew recovery. Navigation and room models may use a simple small graph structure as a lookup, but facts, tasks and lifecycles remain ECS entities with common components.

For both sections, use original titles, drawings, balance and scripts. Games here serve as references for game loops, rather than as a source of copied resources.

## Valuation under the selection contract

Qualitative scale from [evaluation contract](00-evaluation-contract.md): 0 - absent, 1 - weak, 2 - noticeable, 3 - defines the game. Grades are ordinal, without a total; “outside ECS” is higher where a significant portion of the risk is in the specialized engine/algorithms.

| Criterion | FTL-like | Cosmoteer-like | Why |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Combination | 2 | 3 | In FTL, the composition of systems is limited to the ship template; in Cosmoteer, a wide range of parts are assembled into your own ship. |
| Overlapping queries | 2 | 3 | The FTL crew works with systems and rooms; Cosmoteer adds supplies, physical movement, roles, repairs and modular damage. |
| Lineup changes | 2 | 3 | FTL has a dynamic crew, hazards, and upgrades; Cosmoteer also has editable geometry with the destruction/separation of the ship. |
| Events and phases | 3 | 3 | Both have visible tactical consistency; FTL also contains a variety of text meetings/route events. |
| Relationships | 2 | 3 | Both have ship-room-crew and crew-destination-target, but Cosmoteer has more mutable links between modules, carryable resources, and ship parts. |
| Suitability of the first cut | 3 | 2 | FTL can rely on a fixed layout and short route; Cosmoteer requires a small but real construction kit. |
| Load outside ECS | 1 | 3 | Fixed FTL rooms reduce graph cost; Cosmoteer requires an editor, geometry, navigation, collision, rendering and, for the full product, network support. |
| Novelty regarding arena | 3 | 3 | Both versions add strategic control of the ship and crew to direct arena combat; Cosmoteer also checks the graph geometry and dynamic composition of the modules. |

**An array of structures with a short update?** Yes, both cuts can be made on an array of structures. For a small FTL-like it might be even simpler and more direct. ECS is justified as an application structure if real independent systems require overlapping components, and new crew/module capabilities do not require changing the central switch. For Cosmoteer, this support for change is more useful over time, but does not in itself replace the graph solver. This is not a statement about speed.

**Isn't the solution to the main problem hidden somewhere else?** In Cosmoteer - more often than not, yes: checking topology during construction, proximity, path finding, hit calculation, collisions, reachability, ship division and interior rendering. It is necessary to draw a clear boundary between ECS actors/modules and a specialized spatial index. In FTL, crew pathfinding is limited to a small, fixed pattern, which helps keep it simple; however, encounter, ship, weapon, and event tables may become the main work of expanding a campaign.

**Will there remain a recognizable game after the reduction?** One battle - even with a pause, crew management and power supply - is a complete ship-combat prototype, but not an FTL clone: there is no run between nodes, where the player chooses risk, saves losses and applies loot. Therefore, a short mini-run is proposed with the reuse of several templates without a plot catalog. Cosmoteer-like requires placement of units before launch, their different roles in battle, assignment of tasks to the crew and targeting of individual modules. If the editor is excluded, the slice loses the main difference of this hypothesis.

**Are transactions, inspection and snapshots necessary?** Transactions are useful for a real game operation like buying a module with scrap, applying a layout, or simultaneously spending a resource and queuing the work: the interface should see either a full committed result or a failure. The inspector is useful to check why the module is de-energized or the crew is idle. Independent event readers are useful for combat log, outcome and surveillance updates. None of these APIs should be implemented just for coverage.

FTL should not get first place in maximum ECS just because it has crew and ships. Three rival reports provide stronger options under different criteria:

| Competitor | What does FTL give up? | Why FTL is still convenient |
|---|---|---|
| [Space Station 14](07-space-station.md) | There are significantly more intersections “actor ↔ object/tool ↔ container ↔ door/car ↔ environment ↔ round.” The official ECS guide explicitly describes data components and behavior in entity systems; examples show the common `Damageable`, `PowerReceiver` and `PlayerControllable`. This is the strongest published ECS project contract among these candidates. | If you want to maintain a full social round, multiplayer and atmosphere, the station needs large-scale host/solver parts. FTL micro-run is easier to make into a complete single-player game, but not stronger in terms of pure ECS coverage. |
| [Dwarf Fortress / RimWorld / ONI](03-colony.md) | DF benefits from the raw breadth of world systems and story; RimWorld provides needs, jobs, inventory, relationships and incidents with lasting consequences. There are more families of states than a fixed ship layout. | Labor scheduler, reservations, multi-agent AI and pathfinding can become the central product. Ludeon has officially announced that RimWorld's pathfinding has been redesigned as a batch/multithreaded system; this confirms the real cost of the solver, not the internal ECS architecture of the game. |
| [Baba Is You / Opus Magnum / The Incredible Machine](10-rule-puzzles.md) | Baba is stronger for precise custom action "move rule → add/remove behavior": creature properties are directly changed by active rules, then they can be queried by movement, obstacles and victory. This is clearer than in FTL, reveals the runtime composition of components and dispenses with the combat physics of ships. | FTL better shows the long-term crew-room-job relationship, multiple phases of continuous simulation and independent life cycles of weapons/projectiles/hazards. In Baba, attitude and cleanup can be weak, and after adding a broad rule language, parser/content becomes a new risk. |

I cross-checked the three supporting claims of competing reports against official materials. The SS14 documentation directly describes common data components and individual entity systems (including common `Damageable` and `PowerReceiver`); This is a source about the ECS practice of the project itself. ([SS14 ECS guide](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html)) Ludeon says that in update 1.6 pathfinding has become batch and multi-threaded; this shows a separate algorithmic load, but does not prove ECS in RimWorld. ([Ludeon, update 1.6](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/)) The official Baba page and the creator's report confirm rule blocks and queries like “which objects have this rule?”; the report also says that the system is mainly implemented in Lua with tables. This is a strong example of game semantics, similar to queries, but not ECS architecture. ([Baba Is You Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/736260/Baba_Is_You/), [Arvi Teikari, GDC slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/presentations/Reading%20the%20rules_Teikari_Arvi.pdf))

Bottom line for the coordinator: **Cosmoteer remains the stronger of the two ship references for a wide ECS scenario; FTL remains simpler with the first ship-sim cut, but the pure maximum of ECS is not proven here**. When choosing between ship sim, station sim and Baba direction, one must ask whether the first thing you want is (a) a rich network of long-lived relationships and responsibilities, (b) a provable data-oriented ECS consumer, or (c) visible dynamic changes in the composition from a single player turn. It doesn't boil down to just one overall GPA.

## Link to current Bendvy

The normal [App Bendvy](../../../bendvy/docs/reference/ordinary-app.md) registers systems with declared capabilities and executes them in the order of registration. This is suitable for the clearly visible phases of an FTL-like combat or a Cosmoteer-like chain of "shot → damage → hazard → task → movement/repair", but the schedule does not automatically provide parallelism. Component values can include affine `Type` payloads via owner-preserving bindings, however regular repeatable queries `Args` and `Out` are limited to `Data`; It's preferable to keep crew profile, health, position, targets and system parameters in repeatable data without turning every body into a capture.

Systems gain access through declared capability APIs; the world is transmitted affinely, so you cannot build a model where several alias links freely change the ship. Deferred structural commands become visible only on an explicit barrier. This means that the projectile or entity created during repair must appear on the selected phase boundary, so that the new object does not accidentally receive unnecessary updates in the same tick. Transactional failure rolls back the ECS-native changes of the failed system, but host IO and individual capture changes are not included in the same rollback; sounds, UI wins and external effects are published based on committed observation. Detailed current boundaries are formulated in [SPEC](../../../bendvy/docs/SPEC.md) and [ordinary App guide](../../../bendvy/docs/reference/ordinary-app.md).

The World snapshot is not a complete schedule checkpoint. Queues, event-reader cursors, and runtime captures require separate policies; basic/graph save fails when structural commands are pending. It is better to start the first slice again using an explicit seed or design separately the required format for saving the ship and the campaign. Do not promise an exact restoration of the battle from the World snapshot.

Bendvy does not automatically provide a renderer, input, audio, network playback, spatial index, physics/collision, navigation, or editor. This especially affects Cosmoteer: if their build consumes the bulk of the first game, the host capability metric will be chosen rather than the ECS. In FTL-like, the rendering of the room and the control interface remain, but the geometry of the ship can be taken from two static diagrams. The existing [arena](../../../bendvy/examples/arena/README.md) already demonstrates projectiles, movement, damage, pickups and chains; The ship slice should add crew tasks, repairs, food, objective assignment, and the party's strategic outcome.

## Hypothesis rejection conditions and confidence limits

**Refuse Cosmoteer-first for the current version of the project**, if before the completed “built a ship → fought a battle → repaired/lost/won” a full-fledged 2D physics engine, a universal live-editor with undo/blueprint, large-scale pathfinding, a complex fire/heat cellular solver or cooperative synchronization are required. In this case, FTL-like with fixed rooms retains most of the ECS solutions and reduces the cost of infrastructure.

**Refuse the ECS claim for both** if all the slice logic remains the only `ShipKind/EntityKind` in one procedure, the component composition does not change participation in the systems, and the communications between the crew and targets do not have an observable life cycle. Then the example only proves the possibility of realizing space combat.

Confidence is high in comparing visible mechanics and UI/host costs because official sources detail the editor, crew and damage of Cosmoteer and the basic game loop of FTL. Confidence is limited in the level of actual ECS coverage: it is inferred from published mechanics, not source code analysis of commercial games. This hypothesis does not contain any measurements, perf conclusions, architectural statements about the originals or checks of the future gaming slice.
