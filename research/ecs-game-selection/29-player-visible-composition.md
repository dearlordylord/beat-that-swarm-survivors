# 29 - Can the player see the composition?

## How to read the question “maximize ECS”

This is a separate check of interpretation, not a revision of the rankings in [the summary report ](REPORT.md). Here I distinguish two goals:

1. **Development Stress Consumer:** How many independent ECS responsibilities, requests, relationships, events and lifecycle boundaries does the game actually create.
2. **Custom composition demo:** Is it possible to understand from the game why an object can or cannot do something, how several of its parts/abilities are connected, and what exactly changed after the action.

Official developer descriptions confirm the game mechanics listed below and some direct game affordances. They do not measure player understandability or prove that a person can see the internal ECS architecture. “Smarter” below refers to a more direct connection between the condition described to the player and the consequence according to published materials, rather than the result of a user test or taste rating. I did not collect game screenshots or run the games themselves.

## What do the games themselves give the player?

| Candidate | Source confirms | What the composition shows the player and what it doesn’t show |
|---|---|---|
| **SS14** | The official description talks about roles with unique equipment, inventory, starting the reactor and eliminating the consequences of the accident. The official developer guide shows an example of the `InteractUsingEvent`'s handling of the component entity system, but this is a description of the architecture, not the player interface. ([SS14](https://spacestation14.com/about/about/), [official ECS manual](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html)) | **Confirmed:** the role has special equipment; the player manages inventory and the reactor, and an emergency requires actions such as repairs. **Not supported by primary sources in this study:** how playing in a particular UI explains the availability of an action, the desired ability, or the reason for failure. Different abilities overlap on objects, doors and characters - this is shown in [mechanics study SS14](07-space-station.md), but does not automatically turn into a visible model of the object's composition to the player. The sources also do not show a general screen of the composition of the capabilities of the object and all the systems that use it. |
| **Streets of Rogue** | The developer describes completing objectives in different ways through traits, items and the environment, giving examples of a doctor with sleeping pills/darts, a bartender talking past guards and hacking computers as an alternative approach. ([Developer page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/512900/Streets_of_Rogue/)) | **Visible:** character, item and environment provide different solutions to one mission, with effects reflected in NPCs and the objective. This shows the gaming alternatives well. **Limit:** the description does not establish how the UI explains a specific unavailable action, or prove that traits are independent ECS components. The component model here remains our design section ([study 14](14-singleplayer-immersive-sim.md)). |
| **Baba Is You** | The official page explicitly states that the rules are represented by physical blocks; changing the text changes the properties of objects or the purpose. The creator's talk describes turning physical words into game rules. ([Official page ](https://store.steampowered.com/app/736260/Baba_Is_You/), [GDC: Reading the Rules](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026590/Reading-the-Rules-of-Baba) | **Visible:** the rule that changes behavior is written on the board; the player changes it with a move and immediately sees a new consequence. This is the most direct example of “why the object is like this now.” **Limit:** it clearly demonstrates one dynamic semantics, not a broad graph of owners, tools, services, roles, environment and reference cleanup. The `ActiveRules`-resource option can still save the game and this causal cycle; physical insertion of each property-marker is optional ([study 10](10-rule-puzzles.md)). |
| **FTL** | Subset Games describes crew orders, power distribution, weapon targeting, and pause for tactical decisions; an example of a shield threat suggests redirecting energy, attacking, or disembarking the crew. ([Subset Games on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/)) | **Confirmed:** players receive specific alternative orders that change the battle outcome; pausing lets them assess the situation and issue new orders. **Limit:** the reviewed description emphasizes tactical orders, upgrades and selecting different ships, not the free module placement described for Cosmoteer. The exact on-screen indicators or reasons why a particular UI action becomes unavailable are not established here. |
| **Cosmoteer** | The developer describes a shape and layout editor, selection of individual modules (weapons, engine, corridor, cabin), autonomous crew with roles/priorities, separate targeting of the module and destruction with the disintegration of the ship. The author describes the crew carrying ammunition/batteries and how a destroyed corridor could cut off the cannon from its ammunition and render it inoperable. In the devlog of update 0.12.0 (2017), the developer separately describes fire through open spaces and doors and extinguishing by the crew with a fire extinguisher. ([Steam developer page ](https://store.steampowered.com/app/799600/Cosmoteer_Starship_Architect__Commander/), [author about physics and damage](https://waltdestler.com/games/cosmoteer/), [devlog 0.12.0 about fire and crew](https://blog.cosmoteer.net/2017/06/cosmoteer-0120-missiles-fires-and-more.html)) | **Visible from the developer description:** players place modules and paths, assign roles, target part of an enemy and observe the loss of a specific ship function/integrity. An example of a causal chain: the corridor is destroyed → the crew cannot deliver ammunition → the gun stops working; this rule and consequence are described by the author, but the specific UI hint about denial is not established in the sources. This gives the clearest correspondence between visually assembled parts, their role and causal effect among the ship variants. **Limitation:** a detailed explanation of some dependencies - crew occupancy, path, ammunition, connectivity after destruction - may require additional indicators; The descriptions studied do not allow us to claim that the interface explains every failure. The price of this visual assembly is separate geometry, physics, crew pathfinding and solvers ([study 08](08-modular-ships.md)). |

## Which of these should you choose?

**Yes, Baba and Cosmoteer can beat the station in composition readability - in different ways.** Baba shows the shortest causal explanation: the text rule is visible on the field and changes with the player's turn. Cosmoteer shows a broader composition of parts: the module, crew path and task are combined into a ship function, then damage has an observable consequence. This is a conclusion about the structure of the game loops described by the developers; the sources are not comparative user studies.

SS14 still wins if "maximum ECS" means the greatest coverage of independent interactions and domains. But the available primary materials do not promise the player a panel that explains the composition as an engineering model, and there is not enough UI evidence to evaluate the explanation of the reasons for the failure. Streets of Rogue clearly describes different ways to complete a task, but published materials are weaker at showing a systematic explanation of each ability. FTL does a good job of linking orders and ship status to combat outcomes, but the developer's description emphasizes orders, upgrades, and different ships rather than the free assembly of modules like in Cosmoteer; The last comparison is a conclusion based on the published set of mechanics, not a test of the entire UI.

The conditional choice therefore remains two-part:

- If you need a **developer ECS consumer with maximum breadth**, SS14 remains a leader in the criteria studied.
- If the goal is a **playable educational demonstration where someone without source code sees capabilities change**, Baba is the shortest, most direct example; Cosmoteer demonstrates visible construction and destruction more broadly if additional host/solver work is acceptable.
- If you need a **middle ground - multiple system solutions in a short single-player mission**, Streets of Rogue remains a candidate, but its advantage is alternatives to the player, not proven transparency of all reasons.

This is not a new overall estimate or a tacit change to the objective function: the final preference depends on whether the user wants to see the width of the ECS development model or understand it through the game screen. The comparison does not run games and does not measure usability.

## A separate idea for a future prototype

If choosing SS14 for breadth, one can **propose** a demonstration overlay: hovering over an object shows its gameplay capabilities in clear words (for example, powered, repairable, airtight), available actions and their reasons, the last rejection reason and consequences in related systems. Such a panel would be a new UI of the clone being designed, and not a feature of SS14, Baba, Cosmoteer, or Bendvy's already implemented gaming UI. For Baba, such an overlay is redundant relative to the rule tiles themselves; for Cosmoteer, part of the compliance is already visible in the modular layout and combat feedback. Until the prototype, it is unknown whether overlay will help the player and whether it will not overload him; this is not a reason to change the research rating in advance.
