# Independent rating of candidates for the fullest ECS demonstration

## Conclusion

For the priority “where natural game logic yields more independent intersecting ECS systems,” my preliminary winner is **Space Station 14**. This is an inference from the game's interactions, not a reward for the fact that the original already uses ECS. SS14 wins because the same crew, tool, door, cabinet, machine and damaged part of the station can simultaneously participate in movement, possession/containers, interactions, damage, power/atmos, tasks and round outcome. The SS14 repository supports the feasibility of this decomposition; it is not the basis for the suitability assessment itself.

The closest alternative choice is **RimWorld** for the breadth of independent game areas and **Cosmoteer** for changing the structure of objects and the intersection of modular systems. I rank RimWorld second: settlers have overlapping needs, health, skills, jobs, items, and relationships. It's inferior to SS14 in this comparison because most of the decisions about *who does what and where* goes through global job assignment and path availability; if these two solvers consume the game loop, the wealth of components remains around the central planner. This conclusion has medium confidence: the source code of RimWorld has not been examined, and the division into ECS systems is our model of the mechanics.

The rating does not select the fastest clone. A full SS14-like product requires a multi-user role and interaction; The minimum solo-engineer level does not save this core. If the team is not ready for this price, this is a reason to change the product goal and re-select a single-player game, rather than rearrange SS14 in the rating of maximum ECS potential.

## Source Facts and Inference Boundary

Verified developer/publisher sources for significant mechanics. The official description of SS14 talks about round-based multiplayer role-play, crew and enemy roles, inventory, reactor and accident consequences; The official ECS guide describes data in components and behavior in Entity Systems ([SS14 About](https://spacestation14.com/about/about/), [SS14 ECS guide](https://docs.spacestation14.com/en/robust-toolbox/ecs.html)). Some sources on the fixed version show tool/use, damage/destruction, hands/containers and atmospheric systems, but this establishes SS14's architecture, not the suitability of the other projects ([SharedToolSystem.cs](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Shared/Tools/Systems/SharedToolSystem.cs#L249-L303), [HandsSystem.cs](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Hands/Systems/HandsSystem.cs#L137-L183), [DestructibleSystem.cs](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Destructible/DestructibleSystem.cs), [AtmosphereSystem.cs](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/971889f18a6b5f14b926d139ba3910b2872e14de/Content.Server/Atmos/EntitySystems/AtmosphereSystem.cs)).

The other verified gameplay facts are not evidence of the original architecture either: Ludeon lists RimWorld's storyteller and simulation of psychology, ecology, combat, climate, diplomacy, relationships, medicine and trade ([RimWorld, Ludeon on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/294100/RimWorld/)); Subset Games describes crew orders, power allocation, target selection and pausing in FTL ([FTL, Subset Games on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/212680/FTL_Faster_Than_Light/)); Walternate Realities describes customizable ship shapes, independently simulated crew, job priorities, targetable modules and ships breaking apart in Cosmoteer ([Cosmoteer on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/799600/Cosmoteer/)); the Streets of Rogue developer describes a procedural city, AI inhabitants and solving missions through character traits, items and the environment ([Streets of Rogue on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/512900/Streets_of_Rogue/)); Baba Is You's publisher explicitly states that physical rule blocks change rules, objects and the level objective ([Baba Is You on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/736260/Baba_Is_You/)).

These pages confirm visible game loops. They do not allow RimWorld, Cosmoteer, Streets of Rogue, FTL or Baba Is You to be declared ECS programs; Their component and system tables below are the design output. For SoR, a comparison with the previous single-player hypothesis is in [14-singleplayer-immersive-sim.md](14-singleplayer-immersive-sim.md), initial candidate analyzes are [03-colony.md](03-colony.md), [07-space-station.md](07-space-station.md), [08-modular-ships.md](08-modular-ships.md) and [10-rule-puzzles.md](10-rule-puzzles.md).

## Separate axes

The scales are descriptive and do not add up to a pseudo-precise overall score. “Confidence of sources” refers to how verifiable game facts and direct architectural information are. “Natural ECS Intersection” describes how different families of objects share common capabilities and multiple game systems. "Beyond ECS" shows the amount of significant work in dedicated solvers/hosts.

| Candidate | Confidence of sources | Natural ECS intersection | Main load outside ECS |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SS14** | High: gameplay and ECS approach are confirmed by the official website, guide and source codes | Very high: common tool/damage/container/status interactions and common crew/object entities in power, atmosphere, health, roundflow and interaction | Very high: multiplayer/net replication, grid/collision, atmospheric and power topology, UI/content |
| **RimWorld** | High for mechanics, low for runtime architecture: there are public gameplay sources, there is no core source in this study | Very high: pawns participate in needs, health, social relations, jobs, hauling, crafting, combat; items and buildings are associated with tasks and supplies | High: global job matching, pathfinding/reachability, map/fields, storyteller/AI and content |
| **Cosmoteer** | High for mechanics, low for runtime architecture | Very high: one crew, module and item overlap with goals, objectives, transfer, damage, power, fire and ship structure; the player changes the composition of the ship | Very high: builder, geometry, connectivity, pathfinding, physics/collision, battle renderer and network with full clone |
| **Streets of Rogue** | High for mechanics, low for runtime architecture | High: actors, statuses, inventory, items, doors/terminals, AI awareness and mission goals interact on the same level; items have different effects on different targets | Medium-high: AI/perception, line of sight, collision/grid, mission generation/authoring and general interaction resolver |
| **FTL** | High for basic gaming solutions, low for runtime architecture | Medium-High: The crew is connected to the room, the system, the order, and the goal; damage connects the ship, the system, the threat and the crew; less variable geometry and families | Medium: compact room graph, tactical UI, crew/enemy decisions, encounter/event tables |
| **Baba Is You** | High for game rules, low for ECS architecture | Average in overall breadth, very high in dynamic on/off properties; few long-lasting relationships, independent families and third-party systems | Low-medium: rule-scanner/parser, grid occupancy, push resolution and level-authoring |

The original ECS architecture of SS14 is a separate line of evidence: it cannot be counted as a product advantage. It only reduces the uncertainty that the proposed system map has a direct game support, and helps test the “ECS vs network/atmos/grid” boundary. If you remove the source code from consideration, SS14 still remains first in terms of breadth of natural intersections, but the advantage over RimWorld narrows to expert inference. In the current Bendvy, network replication, grid/physics, navigation and renderer do not occur automatically, but systems go in a given order; this increases the scope of the project, but does not change the ECS potential rating ([Bendvy selection contract](00-evaluation-contract.md)).

## Independent rating

1. **Space Station 14.** Maximum cross-coverage in a normal station incident: an object in a container becomes a tool in the hand; the character applies it to an object; the same object can be interactive, damaged, powered and airtight; the breakdown then changes the atmosphere, crew health, access, and round objective. There are significant lifecycle/ownership relations and several independent systems that respond to changing states. The main cost is the network, host, and technical solvers; This doesn't diminish the potential of ECS, but it does make the short simplification especially misleading.
2. **RimWorld.** The strongest competitor in terms of actor wealth and independent subject/social flows. But the planner must combine priorities, skills, work, resources, reservations, and reachability into one global allocation. If in the prototype “all systems” are called around one work allocator, it checks the general task simulator, and not the maximally decomposed ECS logic. There is no source architecture to confidently conclude that this risk in the original is actually transferred beyond the boundaries of the component systems.
3. **Cosmoteer.** The strongest dynamic structural composition: the user assembles the ship, the crew assigns work, modules receive targeted damage, fire/breakdown affects functions, loss of connectivity changes the ship itself. This may give more runtime component/relationship churn than RimWorld. Lower in the ranking due to the fact that the main chain of the game contains a large graph-physics layer: routes, proximity, cutting the structure, collisions and combat will require a separate model; cell entities with frequent traversal will easily turn a clone into a demo of a grid/physics solver.
4. **Streets of Rogue.** The most convincing single-player illustration that capability-composition creates a real choice: neutralize/deceive/bypass security or hack the environment; one status or object affects different actor types. But across the entire width, there are fewer long-lived production/ownership relationships and surrounding subsystems than there are in a station, a colony, and a ship under construction. The central `interact(target, action)`/AI resolver can resolve components in trait/item directories. SoR will increase in ranking if the demonstration proves several different systems reacting independently to one goal/event, and not just a large switch interaction.
5. **FTL.** Clean crew–room–system–order communication and clear combat phases give good composition, events and relations. However, the rooms and ship are mostly fixed in a pattern; growth comes through the crew, upgrades and scenarios, not through reassembling the world. This is a convincing ship slice with a noticeable ECS, but a narrower mission than station or colonial life.
6. **Baba Is You.** Best test case for *changing behavior during gameplay*: move word, recalculate rules, add/remove `Push/Stop/Win/You`, then see different result of move and win. This is an unusually straightforward example of dynamic composition, but its overall ECS scope is narrow: almost the entire game is one grid-rule pipeline, few families, few relationships/ownership, and a limited set of phases. If ranked only based on dynamic component changes, Baba would rise to the top; for the requested maximum of the entire ECS surface - no.

The 2-4 order is less robust than first place: the sheer number of game mechanics could place RimWorld above SS14; the dynamics of the ship composition may place Cosmoteer above RimWorld. To a first approximation, SS14 benefits not from the number of APIs or entities, but from the fact that several independent systems regularly use the same capability compositions on different families of objects. Confidence in this comparison is average because the code for five games has not been examined.

## Why is RimWorld's strongest alternative candidate inferior?

RimWorld is the strongest alternative candidate if we understand the maximum as the breadth of independent game states: each settler has different needs, skills, injuries, social connections, schedules, and work priorities; a physical item goes through selection, storage, recipe and use; storyteller creates external circumstances. This is more variety than one fixed-layout FTL campaign or one custom SoR mission. The official devlog confirms that things are physically on the map, stockpile zones have filters/priorities, and carriers move resources between them ([Ludeon, stockpiling](https://ludeon.com/blog/2014/01/alpha-1-released/)). In a more recent devlog, Ludeon reports on batch multi-threaded pathfinding and load balancing of late colonies across systems ([Ludeon, update 1.6](https://ludeon.com/blog/2025/06/announcing-odyssey-and-update-1-6/)).

But breadth does not equal query independence. The central allocator must continuously select a worker and job, confirm the resource/station/access, hold the reserve, handle priority contention, then pass the path and result to other phases. Two colonists can claim a single resource at the same time; the loser of the job allocator changes the needs and outcome of the event. Nearby is pathfinding/reachability, which does not arise from ECS composition. In SS14, a typical action (tool → door/vehicle → damage/power/air → crew status/round outcome) directly crosses independent systems, without the need to reduce all gameplay to one global planning.

This is not to say that RimWorld cannot be implemented by a good ECS. This is the selection criterion for *the most representative consumer*: if the future prototype distributes work in one global update and the rest is services, RimWorld wins in terms of the density of simulated states, but not necessarily in terms of the actual benefit of the composition. Studying its sources or parsing the evidentiary trace may change this place.

## When a small slice turns the rankings around

- **SS14:** one solo engineer and one broken gateway give a visual chain of systems, but remove multiplayer/role-play and social occurrence of tasks. This is a station-emergency prototype, not a sufficient clone of SS14. This candidate would need to accept the role of the crew and the interaction of at least a few players, or explicitly acknowledge the loss of an important part of the game.
- **RimWorld:** one pawn, hunger and movement to food show need and path, but lose work priorities, physical stockpile, task conflict and storyteller incident. It is unfair to rank such a slice as a wide colony ECS.
- **Cosmoteer:** a pre-built ship in one battle eliminates the need for real construction, which means it compares to FTL, not Cosmoteer. When you keep the editor and destructible modular geometry outside of ECS, the cost increases dramatically.
- **Streets of Rogue:** guard + key + exit turns into dungeon combat if you remove hacking, distraction/social bypass, status and AI/mission reaction to a committed event. The mini-level retains identity only when there are several equal decisions.
- **FTL:** one battle arena without pause, power reroute, crew order and route/event decision - just a top-down shooter. One short transition with a crew, food and at least one encounter/loot choice already preserves the defining course of the game.
- **Baba Is You:** Fixed properties and a single goal make the Sokoban level. You need to physically move the rule and allow the changed property to guide the player to a new outcome; otherwise, both the specificity of the game and the main ECS argument are lost.

Consequently, the first-slice rating is different: SoR, Baba and FTL are more conveniently limited to a complete single-player run; this is not a reason to declare them to be maximal ECS examples. Maximizing the ECS surface means preserving the characteristic system and relational circuits even if the host/network part is more expensive.

## Three gaming tricks for the SS14 winner

These demos test the player's product output rather than the appearance of specific component types. All require at least two interacting crew roles so that acceptance does not slide into a lonely engineering API demo.

1. **Repairing the airlock changes the station.** The engineer takes a suitable tool from the container and repairs the damaged powered airlock while the second player monitors access/room. The player must simultaneously see the status of the tool/use, the restoration of the door/power, the change in the passage, and the termination of the dangerous effect; The log/incident records success after the action is taken. When replacing the door with another car being repaired, the tool remains usable, but the result changes according to the function of the target.
2. **Injury changes the crew's ability to act.** One character is injured; a medic takes an item from a cabinet, carries it over and provides treatment. The UI communicates the effect on health/status and allows the wounded to return to duty or get to evacuation. Transfer, application, consumption/condition of a medical item and treatment should work according to ownership relationships and general health/item systems, and not the specific scenario of one type of character.
3. **A breach creates a choice and a round outcome.** An external incident damages the station boundary; air/hazards change, crew experience the consequences, and alarm/round-objective systems are informed independently. Players can be assigned to isolate and rescue the wounded, or begin evacuation; the result is obvious in the state of the rooms/characters and who made it to the shuttle. Win/loss comes from the committed game state, not the text shown before the decision.

If these three game chains reduce to `StationEventKind` with a large `switch`, or each result requires a specialized global procedure, the solution will not be confirmed. Then it makes more sense to choose RimWorld for a wide live simulation, Cosmoteer for building/modifying the structure, or SoR for single object solutions - depending on which part of the ECS the user wants to test.

## The unknown that can change the choice

SS14 is still leading with moderate confidence. Architectural sources exist, but by themselves they do not provide a metric for natural coverage; air profile, power topology and net replication are a separate technical cost. RimWorld can come out on top if job assignment, pathing and social change can be carried out as several meaningfully independent systems with visible common requests. Cosmoteer can come out on top if destroying/rebuilding the ship produces a stronger component/relation churn than specialized topology/physics steps. SoR can grow if the interpretation of tool/trait/target interactions is divided into capability queries, rather than one universal action. None of these issues can be solved by marketing pages alone.

The work remains an independent analytical rating: the original ECS mechanics of most candidates were not audited, the code and game builds were not changed, and benchmarks and tests were not run.
