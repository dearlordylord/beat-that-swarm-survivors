# Adaptive chip-metal music

`../music.bend` owns the common 132 BPM score, MIDI pitches, four arrangement bands, smoothing, hysteresis and latest-target bar commitment. `game.bend` observes nearby enemies (weighted by type and proximity), hostile projectiles and the player's health. Audio observations happen outside ECS advancement.

The score combines a square bass, short pulse riffs/power fifths, a lead, kick, snare, noise hats and crashes. Four themes (Iron March, Crypt Gallop, Ash Waltz and Night Engine) have distinct harmony, riffs, leads and kick patterns, each with four tension arrangements. Ash Waltz uses a three-step riff inside the common 4/4 transport. Stage transposition adds variation. A run seed chooses themes at complete 32-bar phrase boundaries (about 58 seconds at 132 BPM). Alternating disjoint theme pairs prevents consecutive repeats; changing theme never resets the tension band. Browser seeds come from host randomness; native seeds come from the host clock once per run. Pause retains the seed and musical position. Rising danger adds subdivisions, denser kicks and lead layers. This is procedural composition and synthesis, not a neural music service or a prerecorded track.

There is one replaceable target band, never a queue of transitions. On a bar boundary, the hosts use the latest available world observation, including a fresh boundary observation. Ordinary danger rises by at most 12 points per 100 ms observation, with an eased approach to its target. From calm, the first band needs roughly three seconds of sustained ordinary pressure; heavier arrangements need longer. Raw danger of at least 900 bypasses that delay; audible changes still wait for the next bar. Entry thresholds are 325/650/900, with exit thresholds of 240/560/800. Distant ordinary enemies contribute 3 points each, nearby enemies 30; enemy type still scales the threat. Half health alone contributes 125 points and does not force a combat band. These are initial tuning choices, pending real-run telemetry. The small core laws prove latest-target selection and retention between boundaries; a mutant that retains the old band is rejected. They do not prove the complete audio drivers or musical quality.

Browser `audio.mjs` schedules the shared notes with Web Audio on an independent 25 ms timer, preserving the musical position through pause/reward/mute. It delays scheduling a new bar until within 30 ms. Music and effects have separate voice budgets; browser instrument synthesis remains JavaScript.

Native `native_audio.bend` renders bounded interleaved stereo blocks at 22,050 Hz, with ten voices, into the existing Bend Audio effect. The target queue is 3,072 frames (about 139 ms). New bar decisions wait until no more than 1,024 frames (46 ms) remain queued. Already queued sound cannot be cancelled by this API, so latest-target means latest at generation, with that bounded playback horizon. Native silence/pause does not keep filling the ring. Ordinary pause retains the musical cursor; a new run resets it. Short event cues remain available while a reward pauses music.

Validation receipts and three eight-second listening sketches are in `../../../../bend-games/research/adaptive-chip-metal-20261024/`. Offline Web Audio rendering verifies nonzero, unclipped output and relative density; it is not a physical-speaker audition. The native synthesis fixture exercises stereo packing, lifetime/cap/clock/queue rules and a ten-voice pure-synthesis benchmark. It does not open an audio device. No new Bend runtime, WASM backend or ECS behavior is required.

The music checks cover all four themes on every stage and intensity, seeded phrase stability/non-repetition, ordinary rise delay, emergency response, browser pause/arrangement retention, and native transport/PCM fixtures. Additional laws prove theme-pair range and separation and the emergency rise branch; they do not prove the complete pseudo-random selector or subjective musical quality.
